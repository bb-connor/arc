# Competitive analysis: who else sells delegated authority for agents

This document maps the products, projects, and standards that sell or specify delegated authority for AI agents: who authorized an agent, how much authority it holds, under what conditions, and what evidence remains afterward. It sets each one against Chio's current state, names the direct threats with counters, marks where Chio should and should not compete, and gives a verdict on the positioning thesis. It is written for Chio maintainers. Deeper background sits in [01-nvidia-stack.md](01-nvidia-stack.md) (the NVIDIA side), [02-chio-today.md](02-chio-today.md) (Chio's state), [03-overlap-matrix.md](03-overlap-matrix.md) (the dimension-by-dimension overlap), [04-integration-design.md](04-integration-design.md) (the seams), and [06-strategy-and-roadmap.md](06-strategy-and-roadmap.md) (the plan). This document does not depend on them.

## 2. Comparison dimensions

Ten dimensions decide whether a product can answer "who authorized this action, how much authority was delegated, under what conditions, and can the chain be verified".

1. **Identity**: who the agent and its sponsor are, and who issues that identity.
2. **Delegation chains**: whether authority passes through more than one hop as a recorded chain.
3. **Semantic attenuation**: whether each hop can only narrow, checked over resources, actions, and arguments rather than scope strings.
4. **Budgets**: invocation, rate, and spend ceilings, and whether they survive sessions and hops.
5. **Approvals bound to exact actions**: whether a human approval binds to the specific call, its arguments, and an expiry.
6. **Per-action signed receipts**: a signed record per decision that a third party can check.
7. **Cross-organization verification**: whether a party that does not run the issuer's infrastructure can verify the chain.
8. **Hardware evidence binding**: whether authority or evidence is tied to attested hardware or runtime measurements.
9. **Formal evidence**: machine-checked statements about the policy or the authority mechanism.
10. **Money**: monetary authority as part of delegation, and settlement.

## 3. Threat model: NVIDIA itself

NVIDIA's stack touches authority in three places: OpenShell contains the workload, Sentry is meant to watch the node's path to the model, and SAW and ODIS describe where authority comes from (VERIFIED for the published descriptions). NVIDIA is also building its own authority layer, the Agent Policy Fabric (APF) (INFERRED from a job post, meeting notes, and a public prototype). Each surface below can absorb part of what Chio sells.

### DOCA identity governance

- "The DOCA gateway complements this behavioral protection with identity governance, continuously verifying each agent's identity and delegated authority to ensure it operates within its assigned scope." (VERIFIED, [NVIDIA developer blog, 2026-09-28](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-open-agent-safety-platform-a-reference-for-continuous-in-silicon-agent-monitoring/))
- Sentry is a reference system design. The launch lists only OpenShell software and skills as available and gives no date for Sentry (VERIFIED, [NVIDIA press release, 2026-09-28](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/open-agent-safety-platform)).
- No public source names the credential the gateway accepts, the delegated-authority artifact it verifies, its issuer, or an API (VERIFIED). The DOCA 3.5.0 documentation lists no gateway service and no BlueField-4 platform (VERIFIED, [DOCA 3.5.0 services, updated 2026-09-01](https://networking-docs.nvidia.com/doca/archive/3-5-0/doca-services); [DOCA 3.5.0 general support, updated 2026-09-02](https://networking-docs.nvidia.com/doca/archive/3-5-0/general-support)). It lists DOCA 3.6 LTS for October 2026 and DOCA 4.0 for January 2027 (PROPOSED, [DOCA 3.5.0 changes and new features, updated 2026-09-02](https://networking-docs.nvidia.com/doca/archive/3-5-0/changes-and-new-features)).
- "Attested telemetry" has no published signer or schema. DOCA Argus, rated Beta, emits JSON or syslog with no signature field. A single BlueField DPU running Argus monitors only the compute node it is attached to (VERIFIED, [DOCA Argus guide, updated 2026-09-11](https://networking-docs.nvidia.com/doca/archive/3-5-0/doca-argus-service-guide)).
- NVIDIA says Sentry enforces "the OpenShell policy in silicon" (VERIFIED, developer blog above). It names no policy language of its own (INFERRED). Its launch materials say nothing about budgets, payments, settlement, or delegation across organizations (VERIFIED, developer blog and press release above).
- NVIDIA documents SPDM device attestation for BlueField-3 and does not list BlueField-4 (VERIFIED, [NVIDIA Device Attestation v6.0, 2026-08-31](https://networking-docs.nvidia.com/dpunicattestation)). How Sentry would bind agent evidence to device attestation is not documented.
- The only NVIDIA document that names artifacts for a DPU path is SAW. It marks DPU acceleration "optional, future". It says that path implements the same signed policy bundle and OCSF contracts, with identity and delegation from enterprise IAM or ODIS and enterprise PKI as signing roots (VERIFIED, [SAW security and governance model, last updated 2026-06-28](https://docs.nvidia.com/enterprise-reference-architectures/secure-agent-workspace-reference-design/latest/security-and-governance-model.html)).
- Partners are positioning for the slot (INFERRED). Palo Alto Networks plans an agent identity broker on BlueField through Idira (PROPOSED, [Palo Alto Networks blog, 2026-09-28](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/blog/2026/09/securing-ai-agents-at-scale-with-nvidia/)). Xage feeds entitlement-delegation events into DOCA (PROPOSED, [Xage release, 2026-06-01](https://xage.com/press/xage-security-supercharges-its-just-announced-zero-trust-for-agentic-ai-solution-with-nvidia-vera-bluefield-4-stx-security-innovations)). EQTY Lab claims DPU-held keys that notarize Argus events (PROPOSED, [EQTY Lab blog, 2026-06-01](https://www.eqtylab.io/blog/verifiable-knowledge-the-third-pillar-of-trust-for-the-agentic-ai)).
- NVIDIA's VP of enterprise AI said "In a lot of cases, just using OpenShell on CPUs is honestly good enough" (REPORTED, [VentureBeat, 2026-09-28](https://venturebeat.com/infrastructure/nvidias-open-agent-safety-platform-bets-agents-cant-police-themselves-so-the-infrastructure-has-to)).

**Threat.** If the gateway ships verifying APF bundles or ODIS Delegation Records, a Chio capability reaches silicon only after translation into NVIDIA's format (INFERRED). Chio has no NVIDIA, BlueField, or DOCA code. Its attestation verifier families are Azure MAA, AWS Nitro, Google, and a generic enterprise verifier ([`crates/core/chio-core-types/src/runtime_attestation.rs:25-30`](../../../crates/core/chio-core-types/src/runtime_attestation.rs#L25-L30)) (shipped).

**Counter.** Write no DPU code until DOCA 4.0 publishes an interface. Project Chio grants into OpenShell policy with an explicit loss report, since Sentry is said to enforce that policy. Keep the verifier multi-format. Ask NVIDIA which artifact the gateway verifies and whether an external issuer can be registered.

### Secure Agent Workspace delegation records

- SAW is a reference design; every page reads "Last updated on Jun 28, 2026" (VERIFIED, [SAW overview, last updated 2026-06-28](https://docs.nvidia.com/enterprise-reference-architectures/secure-agent-workspace-reference-design/latest/what-is-secure-agent-workspace.html)).
- It defines a signed per-engagement delegation record that binds the sponsor, workspace, logical agent, and runtime credential to task, scoped resources, tools, duration, approval mode, revocation, and audit references. Child records "can only narrow"; a child holding {A,B} cannot delegate {A,C}. The runtime enforces the record at every tool call, and the sponsor can revoke it (PROPOSED; text VERIFIED, SAW overview above).
- SAW specifies no wire format, signature algorithm, issuer key, owner component, or maximum lifetime. Its control-surface contracts table gives the record no implementing component (VERIFIED, [SAW security and governance model, last updated 2026-06-28](https://docs.nvidia.com/enterprise-reference-architectures/secure-agent-workspace-reference-design/latest/security-and-governance-model.html)).
- SAW says identity and delegation are "consumed from enterprise IAM / ODIS rather than owned". Its dependency list for that control names "ODIS as the spec stabilizes; identity broker; TPM / SEV-SNP / TDX attestation provider" (VERIFIED, security and governance model above).
- SAW's audit contract is OCSF emission to an enterprise SIEM (VERIFIED, security and governance model above). That page specifies no log signing, tamper evidence, or retention (REPORTED, same page).
- SAW states three times that agent authority is a policy-defined subset of the sponsor's permissions, "never a separate authority", evaluated "centrally at the runtime layer". Tool-level authorization sits in its credential proxy (VERIFIED, [SAW enterprise tool access model, last updated 2026-06-28](https://docs.nvidia.com/enterprise-reference-architectures/secure-agent-workspace-reference-design/latest/enterprise-tool-access-model.html)).
- SAW's deployment tiers call for "centralized FinOps integration with budget gates" and specify no metering or budget mechanism (PROPOSED; text VERIFIED, [SAW deployment tiers, last updated 2026-06-28](https://docs.nvidia.com/enterprise-reference-architectures/secure-agent-workspace-reference-design/latest/deployment-tiers.html)).
- SAW excludes acting as an identity provider, serving other users, agent-to-agent platforms, cross-agent coordination, and service agents with no human principal. It is a single-enterprise design, and no page mentions cross-organization delegation or federation (VERIFIED, [SAW: what it is not, last updated 2026-06-28](https://docs.nvidia.com/enterprise-reference-architectures/secure-agent-workspace-reference-design/latest/what-it-is-not.html)).
- Neither public implementation ships Phase II. The Red Hat validated pattern lists signed policy bundles, credential proxying, per-tool enforcement, and OCSF audit as unimplemented (VERIFIED, [validated pattern alignment document, 2026-09-27](https://github.com/validatedpatterns-sandbox/secure-agent-workspace/blob/31364b10d81e61057b50cdf22c0cdab403fb5f0a/docs/architecture-alignment.md)). Red Hat holds 3 of the 13 OpenShell maintainer seats (VERIFIED, [OpenShell@acbac9c `MAINTAINERS.md`](https://github.com/NVIDIA/OpenShell/blob/acbac9cb795094986cbab016bfd0352d980b17f6/MAINTAINERS.md)).

**Threat.** SAW's language places any standalone authority product outside the design's vocabulary. Red Hat and IBM can fill Phase II in-family with Keycloak or IBM Verify, Vault, and OpenShift GitOps (INFERRED).

**Chio today.** `chio.capability.v1` carries scoped tool and resource grants with `issued_at` and `expires_at` ([`crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/token.rs:120-161`](../../../crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/token.rs#L120-L161)) (qualified). Its delegation links only narrow ([`crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/attenuation.rs:64-82`](../../../crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/attenuation.rs#L64-L82)) (qualified, P1). Revoking any presented ancestor rejects the token ([`crates/kernel/chio-kernel/src/kernel/validation.rs:718`](../../../crates/kernel/chio-kernel/src/kernel/validation.rs#L718)) (qualified). The token has no task binding, sponsor identity, approval-mode field, or audit reference, and its issuer and delegator are bare public keys (shipped). Approval thresholds are grant constraints instead ([`crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/scope.rs:345-349`](../../../crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/scope.rs#L345-L349)) (shipped). An enterprise principal exists per session ([`crates/core/chio-core-types/src/session/auth.rs:109-135`](../../../crates/core/chio-core-types/src/session/auth.rs#L109-L135)) but is not bound into the capability (shipped).

**Counter.** Root every capability in the sponsor's IdP grant through an `originating_authorization_ref`, so Chio carries a subset of the sponsor's authority rather than a separate one. Propose a Chio-based Phase II to the Red Hat pattern only after the interceptor exists.

## 4. Threat model: ODIS and the identity providers

### ODIS

- ODIS is an "Unapproved contributor draft" by three NVIDIA authors (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:3-4`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L3-L4)). It has no OASIS or CoSAI approval status (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:1129`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L1129)).
- Layer 1 separates a durable Agent Registration Record, which holds ownership, sponsor, and lifecycle state, from a short-lived Agent Runtime Credential issued after attestation (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:109-111`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L109-L111)).
- ODIS requires a per-hop Delegation Record whose minimum fields include `originating_principal`, `originating_authorization_ref`, `task_id`, and `attenuation_profile_ref`; a non-root record also carries `parent_delegation_ref`. Attenuation must be semantic and fail closed: "Lexical scope-string subset is not sufficient unless semantic equivalence is proven" (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:626-627`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L626-L627)).
- ODIS mandates no wire format. It names OAuth Transaction Tokens, IATP capability manifests, and ID-JAG tokens, and accepts "any carrier satisfying" its section 5.2 (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:417`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L417)). ODIS describes IATP as the handshake of AGT's Agent Mesh (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:547`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L547)). A declaration may name the carrier as `other` (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:999`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L999)).
- Approvals must be bound to the Agent Registration Record, task, requested authority, resource audience, constraints, and expiry (ODIS-L2-02) (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:623`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L623)). Per-agent rate limits are a Core requirement (ODIS-L3-03), and ODIS defines no budget or spend control (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:644`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L644)).
- Federated trust across organizations is an Extended-profile SHOULD (ODIS-L1-07) (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:611`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L611)). The Safety profile makes hardware attestation a MUST (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:955`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L955)).
- Audit requires trace identifiers, and tamper evidence is a SHOULD in Core (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:655`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L655)). Hash chaining is a MUST in the Safety profile, and no signed per-action receipt is defined (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:957`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L957)). Issue [#21, opened 2026-09-21](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/issues/21) proposes a separately verifiable signed decision record (PROPOSED).
- Future work lists portable credentials "for cross-organizational collaboration without centralized trust anchors" (FW-01) and an economic layer (FW-04) (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:1153-1156`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L1153-L1156)).
- A component may publish only a role-capability statement; a profile claim needs a complete deployable target (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:909`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L909)). Process-memory key custody cannot support a Core, Extended, or Safety claim without a CT-P4 companion suite version and a passing test log (VERIFIED, [ODIS@148dc41 `RFCs/ODIS.md:1030-1032`](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/ws4-odis/blob/148dc4187139a41325e3c6d6e7533d956bd33144/RFCs/ODIS.md?plain=1#L1030-L1032)).
- Highflame's ZeroID statement finds that an OAuth JWT carrier covers 6 of the 13 Delegation Record MUST fields fully and 3 partially. It proposes a JWT claim profile for the rest (VERIFIED, [ZeroID role-capability statement, merged 2026-09-04](https://github.com/highflame-ai/zeroid/blob/main/docs/odis/role-capability-statement.md)).
- The Agent Passport System draft (revision -04, an individual submission) specifies delegation chains that a verifier rejects when authority widens across scope, spend, depth, or time (VERIFIED, [draft-pidlisnyi-aps-04, 2026-09-28](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-pidlisnyi-aps/)). It also specifies revocation semantics, signed governed-action records, and MCP bindings (VERIFIED, same draft). Its author offers conformance vectors in OpenShell [#3808, opened 2026-09-28](https://github.com/NVIDIA/OpenShell/issues/3808) (VERIFIED).

**Chio today.** `chio.capability.v1` covers most of the Delegation Record but lacks `originating_principal`, `originating_authorization_ref`, `task_id`, and `attenuation_profile_ref` ([`crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/token.rs:120-161`](../../../crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/token.rs#L120-L161)). Its subject is a public key, and its parent binding is a scope hash ([`crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/attenuation.rs:64-82`](../../../crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/attenuation.rs#L64-L82)) (shipped). Product-path receipt signers hold Ed25519 seeds in process memory (shipped). A Vault transit backend exists ([`crates/trust/chio-signing-remote/src/lib.rs:263`](../../../crates/trust/chio-signing-remote/src/lib.rs#L263)) but is wired only into the finding hosted profile ([`crates/platform/chio-control-plane/src/trust_control/finding_hosted_profile.rs:558`](../../../crates/platform/chio-control-plane/src/trust_control/finding_hosted_profile.rs#L558)) (shipped). Chio's spec uses "Agent Passport artifacts" ([`spec/PROTOCOL.md:68`](../../../spec/PROTOCOL.md?plain=1#L68)).

**Threat.** If ODIS settles on a JWT carrier or adopts #21, Chio's token becomes a translation step at every ODIS boundary (INFERRED). "Passport" means three different things across ODIS, Chio, and the Agent Passport System (VERIFIED for the usages).

**Counter.** Publish a role-capability statement that declares process-memory custody. Publish a carrier profile with a digest-bound companion for the four missing fields, and an attenuation profile at an immutable URI plus digest, all pinned to `148dc41`. Co-author #21 instead of forking it. Say "portable trust credential" in standards contexts. Never claim ODIS conformance.

## 9. Threat model: Anthropic Claude Managed Agents

- Claude Managed Agents (beta) keeps the agent loop, permission policies, and MCP-connector calls on Anthropic's side. Permission policies are `always_allow`, `always_ask`, and `auto`; under `auto` the server evaluates each call and the client cannot override its denials (VERIFIED, [Managed Agents permission policies, beta, no visible date](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/permission-policies)).
- Each tool-use event carries `evaluated_permission` (VERIFIED, same page).
- Vaults hold credentials and are workspace-scoped. Session budgets set hard spend ceilings. Subagent threads share the same sandbox, filesystem, and vault credentials (VERIFIED, [vaults, no visible date](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/vaults); [budgets, no visible date](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/budgets); [multiagent orchestration, no visible date](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/multiagent-orchestration)).
- Custom tools run in the customer's worker and fall outside Anthropic's permission policies (VERIFIED, [self-hosted sandboxes, no visible date](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/self-hosted-sandboxes)). For self-hosted sandboxes, "Anthropic's security boundary stops at the sandbox" (VERIFIED, [self-hosted sandbox security, no visible date](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/self-hosted-sandboxes-security)).
- A self-hosted worker authenticates to Anthropic's control plane with an environment key and a per-session secret. No identity that the agent presents to tools is documented (VERIFIED, self-hosted sandbox security above).
- The self-hosted page lists eleven provider guides and none for OpenShell (VERIFIED, self-hosted sandboxes above). The 2026-09-28 announcement describes the Managed Agents and OpenShell layers as enforcing their limits independently; only NVIDIA's text mentions BlueField (VERIFIED, [Claude blog, 2026-09-28](https://claude.com/blog/giving-companies-more-control-over-their-ai-agents-with-nvidia)).

**Threat.** Claude customers may treat authority as solved by the lab control plane plus OpenShell, which leaves Chio only custom tools per sandbox (INFERRED).

**Counter.** Own custom tools and MCP servers wrapped as custom tools, per sandbox. Record Anthropic session and event IDs in receipts. Confirm the worker-in-OpenShell topology with Anthropic before building. Never claim per-thread narrowing, since threads share one sandbox and its credentials.

## 14. Verdict on the authority-kernel thesis

### The thesis as stated

The working positioning thesis presents Chio as the authority kernel for autonomous systems. That category does not survive in NVIDIA contexts. It collides with three facts:

1. SAW says agent authority is "never a separate authority" and is evaluated "centrally at the runtime layer" (VERIFIED, section 3).
2. Microsoft already markets AGT's Agent OS as "the kernel for AI agents" (VERIFIED, section 4).
3. NVIDIA is staffing APF (VERIFIED for the job post). Its planned scope (PROPOSED) would cover most of the single-organization kernel role on the same seam (INFERRED).

Chio's landscape document says "Chio is the only protocol where a single token simultaneously expresses identity ..., authorization ..., economic limits ..., and produces cryptographic proof ... for every decision" ([`docs/reference/COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md:529-532`](../../../docs/reference/COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md?plain=1#L529-L532)). That document was last updated 2026-03-21 (line 5), and no claim-registry row approves the claim. The claim registry says the document should "avoid first-principles claims about external systems" ([`docs/reference/CLAIM_REGISTRY.md:129-130`](../../../docs/reference/CLAIM_REGISTRY.md?plain=1#L129-L130)).

AGT and AP2 falsify the landscape claim at the feature level, although AGT's pieces are weaker or optional by default and AP2 covers payments only (VERIFIED, sections 4 and 6). Keep "kernel" as a component name. Drop it as the category in NVIDIA, SAW, and ODIS contexts. The README's "The kernel your agents answer to." ([`README.md:16`](../../../README.md?plain=1#L16)) should not travel into that material.

### The narrowed thesis and five corrections

The narrowed thesis is a target position; section 11 labels each component. It has three parts. Chio carries authority that originates in an enterprise grant as a capability that can only narrow. It checks that capability against each request's arguments, counts, and budget at the runtime's own enforcement point. It leaves signed evidence, joined to the runtime's record, that a counterparty can verify. It is not per-call policy evaluation as a product and not identity issuance. It survives with five corrections.

1. **Keep per-call argument checks, but do not price them.** OpenShell never matches arguments (VERIFIED). #2025 has no NVIDIA replies, and #2109's draft PR has had no author commits since 2026-07-08 (VERIFIED), so both look stalled (INFERRED). The check is therefore the delivery vehicle and the point where receipts are minted. Lynx, the ODIS harness, COAZ-MCP, Relay guardrails, and MCP 2026-07-28 headers make it common (INFERRED).
2. **"Offline-verifiable lineage" is one hop today.** `validate_delegation_chain_with_trust_root` rejects proof-carrying chains longer than one link ([`crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/attenuation.rs:301-306`](../../../crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/attenuation.rs#L301-L306)), and deeper chains rely on hosted ancestor snapshots (shipped). Say "single-hop portable, deeper chains hosted" until per-hop witnesses ship.
3. **"What evidence remains" needs three outcome states and a runtime binding.** OpenShell documents that a middleware response block does not undo a request that already ran (VERIFIED, [OpenShell@acbac9c `proto/supervisor_middleware.proto:237-239`](https://github.com/NVIDIA/OpenShell/blob/acbac9cb795094986cbab016bfd0352d980b17f6/proto/supervisor_middleware.proto?plain=1#L237-L239)). A receipt must therefore distinguish denied before forwarding, forwarded and answered, and outcome unknown.
   - The claim that evidence is verifiable without trusting the operator holds only in the cross-organization form. In one organization the operator holds the signing key, the gateway registration, and the unsigned OCSF source (INFERRED).
   - The bounded release excludes strong non-repudiation ([`docs/release/QUALIFICATION.md:76-83`](../../../docs/release/QUALIFICATION.md?plain=1#L76-L83)) (qualified).
4. **"Behind OpenShell's chokepoints" covers only part of the traffic.** A middleware selector holds at most 32 host patterns, and `fail_closed` cannot apply to `tls: skip` endpoints (VERIFIED, [OpenShell@acbac9c `docs/how-it-works/policies/schema.mdx:494-496`](https://github.com/NVIDIA/OpenShell/blob/acbac9cb795094986cbab016bfd0352d980b17f6/docs/how-it-works/policies/schema.mdx?plain=1#L494-L496)).
   - The hook inspects HTTP/1.x requests that OpenShell terminates and parses. HTTP/2, HTTP/3, opaque TCP, `tls: skip` traffic, and binary WebSocket messages are excluded (VERIFIED, [OpenShell@acbac9c `rfc/0009-supervisor-middleware/README.md:524`](https://github.com/NVIDIA/OpenShell/blob/acbac9cb795094986cbab016bfd0352d980b17f6/rfc/0009-supervisor-middleware/README.md?plain=1#L524)).
   - Stdio MCP and local shell and file effects never reach the hook (INFERRED). Completeness is operator-attested, not provable.
5. **"ODIS-compatible" means a carrier profile, not conformance.** It means a profile for `chio.capability.v1`, a digest-bound companion for the four missing MUST fields, and a role-capability statement that declares process-memory custody (VERIFIED for the ODIS rules, section 4). "Never a separate authority" means every root capability carries an `originating_authorization_ref` from the enterprise IdP. `chio.capability.v1` lacks that field today ([`crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/token.rs:120-161`](../../../crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/token.rs#L120-L161)) (shipped).

### What would change the verdict

The defensible position is not the concept of delegated authority, which NVIDIA, Microsoft, IBM, Keycard, and Tigera all describe (VERIFIED for their published descriptions). It requires an implementation that is released, formally modeled, and fail-closed (INFERRED). That implementation must carry authority that can only narrow, bound to a runtime's own identifiers, with budgets that cross hops, verifiable by a party that does not run the gateway (INFERRED).

What is qualified today is local and single-organization ([`docs/release/QUALIFICATION.md:67-83`](../../../docs/release/QUALIFICATION.md?plain=1#L67-L83)) (qualified). Offline-portable lineage covers one hop (shipped). Four pieces of roadmap work would turn the position into a durable advantage: per-hop witnesses for multi-hop chains, partition-escrowed budgets, receiver-owned admission in released binaries, and independent key custody per organization (roadmap).

Set a kill-or-continue checkpoint for the relying-party and cross-organization seats at the end of the plan's second quarter (Q2) in [06-strategy-and-roadmap.md](06-strategy-and-roadmap.md). Continue if one relying-party pilot or one design-partner pair converts. Otherwise fall back to operator-side evidence processing in the exporter slot that OpenShell #3224 proposes, and reassess the thesis.

Signals that move the checkpoint earlier (INFERRED):

- Public APF RFCs or code.
- Publication of NVIDIA's OpenShell exporter with signing.
- #2881 or approver attribution landing in a weekly release.
- A DOCA 4.0 gateway interface that names its delegated-authority artifact.
- An ODIS decision on a carrier or on #21.
- AGT turning receipts on by default.
- An OCSF release that carries `digital_signature.value`.
