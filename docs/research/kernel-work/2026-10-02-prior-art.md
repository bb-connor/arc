## The three counterdesigns to write first

These are proposed constructions to test, not claims that complete equivalents
have already been built.

**B1: Conventional independent services.** Each owner runs its own protected
authorization service and durable workflow/ledger. Requests bind stable operation
IDs, exact inputs, local policy, resource allocations and signed outcomes. Use
the same provider lookup, result checker and settlement rail as Chio. Permit
ordinary extensions, carefully implemented cross-store bridges and explicit
approval/recovery records. The design may share a commitment authority when the
Chio profile also relies on one.

**B2: Capability contracts.** Use Agoric's capability/durable-contract concepts
and protected asset allocation, adding a qualified external-effect adapter where
needed. Ask which properties require shared execution/settlement infrastructure
and which can remain local to owners. Do not compare an on-chain-only fragment
with Chio's complete host and then claim an intrinsic impossibility.

**B3: Recoverable enforcement.** Use pinned OpenAPPA with an equally qualified
host boundary, exact provider contracts, durable operation handling and an
ordinary payment/checker service. Allow engineering improvements, including
artifact persistence and owner-specific policy handling where needed. Record
their implementation and trust cost instead of forbidding them. Compare policy
meaning explicitly: identical label variant names do not imply identical algebra.

A counterdesign may reproduce the same rule in different code. That can refute
an exclusivity claim while supporting the value of a general contract. The next
question becomes whether Chio's realization changes trust, coordination, useful
progress, or repeated integration materially. Equal guarantees and comparable
cost must be recorded as a negative research result.
