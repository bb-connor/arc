# Paper 2 Claim Audit: Bilateral Receipt Admission

Paper: `docs/papers/bilateral-receipt-admission/` at HEAD `fe5657020c`. Finder IMPL2 (implementation, wire format, gates, rejection codes, benchmarks, modules), adversarially verified in the resumed run; findings from FORM, HARN, XREF and PROSE that bear on P2 are cross-referenced by id. Raw findings are in the appendix and in `findings.json`.

## Verdict

P2 is a May 2026 draft (PDF dated 2026-05-19, last content commit before the June crate move) that describes a construction which does not exist in the repository in the form stated. It has the shape a foundational document should have: one named primitive, a worked envelope, a canonicalization rule, an attack-by-construction section, plainly asserted claims. Almost every checkable detail in that shape is false at HEAD, and several were false when the paper was written. The paper cannot be submitted, cited, or circulated as a description of Chio. Its good instincts should be carried into the surviving paper after being rewritten against the code.

The blockers, each confirmed by at least two independent finders:

1. **Gate order is inverted.** The paper says the verifier never reaches its six gates until both signatures verify. At HEAD `verify_chio_bilateral_dsse_envelope_inner` verifies both Ed25519 signatures last, after twelve structural checks; the operational verifier consults verifier-owned peer pins before any byte-level check. The paper contradicts itself on this between sections 3, 5, 6 and 7. (IMPL2-01, PROSE-03, XREF-02, HARN-22)
2. **The five rejection codes do not exist.** No code named noncanonical-payload, predicate-type-mismatch, signer-reuse, stale-lease, or subject-digest-mismatch occurs anywhere in crates or spec. The shipped taxonomy is 16 `VerifierError` codes plus the `BilateralCoSigningError::code` mapping, most structural failures being `CanonicalJson(String)` values distinguished by message prefix; the normative spec lists 15 codes and disagrees with the code on five. The paper's "bundling" rationale for G4 is a non sequitur, and its log2(5) oracle bound describes nothing in the artifact, whose error values embed presented and expected field values. (IMPL2-05, IMPL2-02, IMPL2-07, PROSE-02, PROSE-11, PROSE-13, HARN-10)
3. **The wire format is fictional.** The worked envelope uses camelCase fields, a `lease` object with epoch and expiry, `signerKids`, and a statement type string that the shipped verifier rejects; every predicate struct is `deny_unknown_fields`, so the printed example fails decoding at the first gate. The "ten-field binding tuple" is given five different cardinalities across the abstract, section 3, the figure, its caption, and the example. The shipped predicate is snake_case with an optional 15-field `treaty_binding_ref`. (IMPL2-08, IMPL2-11, PROSE-01, PROSE-04, XREF-03)
4. **The subject digest is not what the paper says.** The paper's central binding argument rests on subjectDigest = SHA-256(JCS(bindingTuple)). At HEAD the subject digest is the SHA-256 of the canonical receipt body resolved from the receiver's own store; the treaty bindings are signed predicate fields, not hashed into the subject; and the strict envelope verifier does not check the digest at all (only the operational verifier does). The sibling-treaty attack is rejected by `chio_treaty_scope_hash_mismatch` in runtime-core, not by a digest gate. (IMPL2-09, IMPL2-10, XREF-03)
5. **Two defended attacks have no mechanism.** No treaty declares a multi-lane witness quorum and the bilateral verifier evaluates no anchor set; the only lane-quorum object is a 55-line Lean model with no Rust binding. No operator-attribution metadata exists; signer independence is key and keyid distinctness only, which is also what the paper's own sections 5 and 9 and P1's PS-A-01 say. (IMPL2-16, IMPL2-13, FORM-15, HARN-01, HARN-02, XREF-05, XREF-06)
6. **The formal claims are wrong in five ways.** The Lean envelope carries an `AdmissionView`, not a receipt id; the trust store has no lease epoch; the four theorems depend on `propext` alone, not on choice and `Quot.sound`; the proof is two tactic lines; and the "six gates collapse into three conjuncts" claim has no artifact on either side. The abstract's sentence that correctness "rests on the conjunction of six gate predicates over canonical bytes, formalized in a Lean module" is false on gate count, on bytes, and on axioms. (FORM-01 to FORM-05, IMPL2-26, HARN-04, HARN-18, PROSE-14, PROSE-24)
7. **The evaluation does not measure the construction.** The 72.051 us figure is a local-policy dispatch bench with no admission hook and a non-federated request, on hardware no retained file records; the treaty-intersection sweep's generator existed once (a hand-rolled loop added at `90a10ee4e2`, deleted with its results at `0b2a024c36`) and nothing at HEAD can regenerate it; the 50-fixture corpus is a kernel-capability golden gate with no DSSE envelope, continuation hash, or subject digest, misdescribed as exercising bilateral gates; the "three denial fixtures" map to different codes in P1's 20-case corpus. (IMPL2-18 to IMPL2-21, HARN-03, XREF-13, XREF-14, PROSE-34)

The completeness critic added one major and several minor defects. "Every admission and denial appears as a canonical receipt" and "signed negative packages" are overclaims: at HEAD a denial produces a signed receipt only when the request reaches the kernel dispatch path through the runtime admission hook; envelope-level and validator denials, including all 20 matrix cases, return typed errors with no receipt, and the closure's "signed negative" material is five deliberately tampered packages (CRIT-01). The wire format does carry enforced per-signer and per-hash algorithm tags, contrary to the limitations section (CRIT-02); the selective-disclosure "issuer-owned key registry" is an in-memory test aid (CRIT-04); the SCITT references are now RFCs 9942 and 9943, not AUTH48 drafts (CRIT-07). The verifier also found that signer reuse yields three different codes at three entry points, and that `bilateral_verifier.rs` describes itself as a partial local verifier that is not full section 7 conformance, which the paper never discloses while calling it strict (IMPL2 verifier notes 1 and 2).

Further confirmed defects: the macOS Endpoint Security "stub" paragraph describes a module that is not in the repository (IMPL2-17, PROSE-09); the clause "exchange canonical bytes through library calls rather than the network" is false given the QUIC co-signing lane in `chio-federation-transport-iroh`, although the evaluated closure does not use that lane and no multi-process scenario is demonstrated, so the headline limitation should be restated rather than deleted (IMPL2-28); BBS is an opt-in feature of a separate crate and its "stub-versus-real disambiguation" is a schema-string check unrelated to the bilateral verifier, and the scheme is the IRTF BBS signature, not the 2004 group signature (IMPL2-14, IMPL2-15, XREF-21); release of reserved state on "any denial or aborted continuation" overstates a best-effort release with fail-closed retention (IMPL2-24, HARN-06); the federated-origin fail-closed path keys off a request field that no in-tree production adapter sets (IMPL2-23); the predicate type is a compile-time constant, not resolved from the treaty (IMPL2-29); the `bilateral_dsse.rs:996` citations point at a file that no longer exists as one file (PROSE-10); `PREDICATE_TYPE_BILATERAL` names the compatibility signature-slice profile, not the strict type (XREF verifier note 1); one cited reference resolves to a different paper (PROSE-05) and the bibliography has degraded author lists (PROSE-17); "anonymized for review" appears fourteen times while the affiliation names the project (PROSE-16, XREF-17).

## What P2 gets right that P1 lacks

These are worth merging into the surviving paper after being rewritten against the code:

- The framing sentence: supply-chain provenance attests how an artifact was built; agent invocations need provenance for how an action was admitted at the receiving kernel. (Section 1)
- The SCITT and COSE-receipt contrast: a two-issuer joint accept at admission time versus an issuer-plus-witness pair that may post-date the statement. (Section 8)
- Rejection codes as protocol surface with signed denial packages in the same schema as admissions. (Sections 3, 6; the real mechanism is `VerifierError::code` and the runtime denial receipt)
- The attack-by-construction format: adversary capability, verifier-visible state, the rejecting gate, residual risk. (Section 7; it must be redone over `TreatyBindingRef` with the real codes)
- The Ed25519-authoritative, BBS-presentation-only policy stance. (Sections 2, 5; keep as one sentence, cite the spec)

## What to do

Retire P2 as a paper. Keep it as an internal design note or fold the standards material into the in-toto proposal in `spec/CHIO_BILATERAL_COSIGN_INVOCATION.md`. Do not circulate it: a reader who checks one gate, one code name, or one field name will find it wrong, and the same reader will then doubt P1. The specific merges into P1 are listed in `05-cross-paper-consistency.md` and `10-roadmap.md`.
