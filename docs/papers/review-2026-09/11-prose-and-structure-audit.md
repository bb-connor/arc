# Prose, Structure, and Reader-Experience Audit

Finder PROSE, adversarially verified. This wave was fact-focused, so taste judgments were kept but marked minor; factual defects surfaced by reading as a first-time reader were promoted and cross-checked against source.

## Verdict

P1 reads as a competent, honest systems paper. A reader knows what the construction is by the third sentence of the abstract and why it matters from the refund example in the first paragraph. The reader is lost in two places: section 3.1, whose noun lists name eight things for the treaty scope and twelve for the predicate without a worked example, and section 4, which presents the Lean model before the reader learns in section 5.4 that it is not an authorization path. The paper's defect is defensive repetition. About 18 percent of its sentences hedge (32 percent in the introduction); the "two keys are not two organizations" disclaimer appears nine times in the body plus the README, claim ledger, and supplementary README; the "Lean does not verify Rust" disclaimer four times; and the introduction ends on a disclaimer. Every hedge is true and several are required by the claim registry, but the paper reads as a rebuttal to a previous review rather than as the statement of an idea. Four of six floats, including the main results table and the assumptions table, are never referenced from the text (PROSE-19, PROSE-20, PROSE-22, PROSE-35).

P2 is not readable as a description of the artifact. Its spine (five rejection codes, a ten-field tuple hashed into the subject, signatures verified first) is false at HEAD; the reader understands what the construction is only at the binding-tuple paragraph of section 3, never gets a concrete scenario, and is lost in section 4 (six gates collapse into three with a self-contradicting justification), section 6 (measures the local-policy path, not the construction), and fourteen deferrals to an anonymized companion. 43 sentences exceed 40 words (the longest 115); "structural(ly)" appears 15 times, "load-bearing" 6, "rather than" 25 (PROSE-01 to PROSE-05, PROSE-15, PROSE-16, PROSE-28).

## Related work both papers miss

A program committee will raise these, one line each: PolicyMaker and KeyNote (Blaze, Feigenbaum, Lacy 1996) are the original "compliance checking of credentials against local policy", which is receiver-owned admission by another name; SDSI and SPKI (RFC 2693) introduced receiver-local names and authorization certificates and are the ancestor of "trust resolved from the receiver's own stores"; Macaroons and Biscuit are attenuable bearer tokens with contextual caveats, the obvious alternative to treaty-bound predicates; UCAN and ZCAP-LD are the capability-token formats agent systems actually use; OAuth token exchange (RFC 8693) and rich authorization requests (RFC 9396) are the enterprise path; SPIFFE federation and Kerberos cross-realm trust are the deployed cross-domain trust models; MCP, A2A, x402, and AP2 are the protocols the repository itself adapts and neither paper cites; SCITT and COSE receipts are missing from P1; OPA is named in P2 without a citation (PROSE-18, PROSE-33). Both bibliographies carry more uncited entries than cited ones (P1 47 of 70, P2 48 of 76); P2's bibliography was derived from P1's and then degraded, with wrong author lists and one identifier that resolves to a different paper (PROSE-05, PROSE-17, PROSE-30).

## Structural recommendations for the surviving paper

- Open with the scenario and carry it through as the worked example; show one real envelope and one real denial receipt, copied from a fixture the harness emits.
- Add a five-line glossary at the top of section 3 (kernel, treaty, ladder manifest and action class, continuation, lease and governance receipt, buyer package) and a one-column table mapping each term to its code symbol and schema id.
- State each disclaimer twice: once in the threat model with its identifier, once in the assumptions table. Delete the others. End the introduction on the contribution, not on a caveat.
- Reference every float from the text; add the request-carries-trust decision node to the admission figure so it matches the seven-step list (PROSE-21).
- Move the bounded model after the implementation, or fold it into the differential-testing question, with one sentence stating its role.
- Delete the "programmable sovereignty" paragraph and the legal-political bibliography, or rename the directory to match the title (PROSE-30). Decide whether the submission is double-blind; if so, use a neutral system name and generic paths, because the project is public (PROSE-29).
