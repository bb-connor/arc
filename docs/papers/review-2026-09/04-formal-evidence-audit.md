# Formal Evidence Audit

Finders FORM (Lean theorems cited by either paper, axioms checked with `#print axioms`, proof depth) and HARN (the non-Lean estate: Kani, Creusot, Aeneas, Apalache and TLA+, Loom, fuzzing, mutation, differential and conformance tests), each adversarially verified. This document answers three questions: what the cited theorems actually prove, what the repository could prove about this path that the papers do not use, and what the papers assert that no harness supports.

The P1 formal story is accurate and proportionate: it calls its model small, states the axioms correctly, and says plainly that Lean does not verify Rust (FORM-09, FORM-12, FORM-17, IMPL1-18). Its only formal gaps are institutional. `ReceiptPredicate.lean` is root-imported and `sorry`-scanned but is absent from `formal/proof-manifest.toml` root modules, `formal/theorem-inventory.json`, `docs/formal/COVERAGE.md`, and the Lean mutation allowlist, so by the repository's own rule the two theorems are not registered release evidence and no gate would notice if `refinesOn` were broken (FORM-10, HARN-05).

The P2 formal story is false in the direction that matters. The paper over-reports its axiom footprint, describes a version of `BilateralAccept.lean` that was replaced on 2026-07-12, calls a definitional bi-conditional "load-bearing" in section 1 and "not load-bearing" in section 4, asserts a six-to-three gate collapse that has no artifact on either side, and misdescribes `essential_preserved_chain` as a theorem that "obliges every amendment to refine the prior predicate set" when its per-step preservation is a hypothesis and its relation runs in the opposite direction (FORM-01 to FORM-06, XREF-01, XREF-08). The reversible-action planning artifact in the same repository already records this criticism of the polity theorems under its "rfl-gate" header; that file itself no longer compiles against the proof root and its headline theorem is `sorry` (FORM-07, FORM-14, XREF-19).

Two Lean-lane weaknesses are real and unpublished. The Treaty theorem set does not pin the `.top`, `.bot`, or `.disj` cases of `supported` and `defined`; six open mutation survivors (issues #1005 to #1010) live there, and the FORM finder reproduced in Lean that survivor #1007 alone lets an unavailable-mode atom become an allow through `neg (disj _ bot)`, a shape no theorem covers (FORM-08). And the two papers cite two disjoint bounded predicate languages (receipt-view predicates in `ReceiptPredicate`, admission-view predicates in `PredicateLang`) that share vocabulary and no theorem (FORM-13).

## The estate the papers do not use

The bilateral, treaty, and continuation path has no Kani harness, no Creusot contract, no fuzz target, no `cargo-mutants` coverage, and no entry in the spec or proof mutation registries (HARN-17). Its evidence is Lean abstraction anchors, bounded Apalache models that touch it at the edges, and an extensive example-based test estate. Neither paper cites the strongest parts of that estate. Ranked by how much each would strengthen the central claim:

1. `crates/tooling/chio-conformance/tests/c2_bilateral_invocation_partial_verifier.rs`: 21 byte-mutation tests mapped to the spec 7.1 codes, run in the workspace gate on every PR. The verifier notes that this suite exercises the partial signature-slice verifier rather than the strict one P1 cites, so it corroborates the code discipline on a sibling verifier; still the best independent evidence of the taxonomy (HARN-12).
2. Kernel tests in `crates/kernel/chio-kernel/src/kernel/tests/federation_cosign.rs`, `chio_runtime.rs`, and `durable_admission.rs`: forged runtime treaty metadata cannot reach dispatch or the cosigner; an unpinned peer fails closed pre-dispatch; the cosigner is never called when local persistence fails. This is the "cannot be bypassed" evidence (HARN-13).
3. Apalache `PostAdmissionDropGuard.tla` (`RetainedIffAborted`, `ReservationConservation`) and `ReceiptBeforeAllow.tla`, whose mirror anchors name `ChioRuntimeAdmissionHook::release_reservations` and `record_chio_receipt_with_federation`, plus the production-path proptest `drop_guard_disposition_table`. These are the bounded models of release-or-retain on abort and receipt-before-allow (HARN-14). They abstract the treaty continuation into an aggregate lease and do not cover crash.
4. DSSE and PAE conformance (`b4_bilateral_dsse_signature_slice`, 13 tests) and the generated `bilateral_dsse_producer_typestate` state machine: PAE construction, preimage non-overlap with the legacy dual-signed receipt, single-subject enforcement, producer ordering (HARN-15).
5. `runtime_admission_policy_exact` and the `signer_*` fail-closed theorems in `PredicateLang.lean` with their hash-enforced anchors on the exact validators P1 describes (HARN-16).

## What the papers assert that no harness supports

- Continuation single-use under crash or concurrent consumption: store-enforced and tested single-process; no crash, reopen, Loom, or per-identifier model exists; the Apalache lease is aggregate (HARN-09). P1 scopes this correctly in its limitations; P2 does not.
- The hook-before-budget ordering and the "kernel first performs its ordinary checks" sentence: true at HEAD, pinned by nothing (HARN-07).
- P2's "rejected structurally rather than detected" and "by construction" wording: backed only by enumerated example tests (HARN-17).
- P2's constant-time idealization: the nightly `dudect` lane does not reach the bilateral verifier (HARN-11).
- P2's error-oracle bit bound: no harness, and the taxonomy it is computed over does not exist (HARN-10).
- P2's multi-lane witness quorum and operator attribution: no mechanism (HARN-01, HARN-02).

## Assumption discipline

The repository's registry (`formal/assumptions.toml`) names fifteen audited assumptions and the claim registry disallows specific phrases. P1 contains no disallowed wording (HARN-19). Its assumptions table covers cryptography, canonical JSON, receiver trust, and key custody in prose but omits the registry ids the path depends on: `ASSUME-OS-CLOCK`, `ASSUME-SQLITE-ATOMICITY` (single-row scope, which is exactly the crash gap P1 excludes), `ASSUME-SUBPROCESS-ISOLATION` (for the zero-invocation observation), and the float-domain scope of `ASSUME-CANONICAL-JSON`; and it relies on a federated-origin-classification assumption that the registry does not track at all (HARN-20). P2's limitations omit the clock, storage, canonical-JSON, and transport assumptions entirely and introduce two premises about mechanisms that do not exist (HARN-21). The critic adds that neither paper cites the registry ids at all, that P2 attributes "Unicode string normalization" to RFC 8785 (neither the RFC nor the implementation normalizes; JCS escapes), and that the typed canonicalization path emits integers above 2^53-1 exactly rather than as doubles, which the assumptions table should name (CRIT-03, CRIT-05).
