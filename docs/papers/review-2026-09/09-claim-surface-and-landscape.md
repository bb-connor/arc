Part A maps what the repository truthfully supports today, in the vocabulary of `docs/reference/CLAIM_REGISTRY.md`. Part B maps the competitive and prior-art landscape. ## Part A: what Chio is today

### Honest one-paragraph description

Chio today is a pre-release Rust kernel (docs/release/RELEASE_AUDIT.md: continue pre-release evaluation, no GA) that mediates tool calls for AI agents: an agent presents an Ed25519-signed capability token whose scope can only be narrowed along a signed delegation chain (attenuation proofs, scope-hash chain binding, basis-point sibling budget splits, caveats); the kernel verifies the token, checks the leaf and every chain ancestor against a local revocation store and, when federation gossip has installed one, against a signed-epoch revocation view with a freshness gate, places a budget hold, runs a fail-closed guard pipeline compiled from HushSpec policy or sandboxed WASM, dispatches to the tool server, and signs a canonical-JSON receipt whose identity is the hash of its own content; receipts are appended insert-only to SQLite and batched into RFC 6962 Merkle checkpoints that the protocol itself classifies as local audit evidence rather than a public transparency log. A pure decision core (verify, resolve, evaluate, sign, plus budget, DPoP, nonce, guard-step, and revocation-snapshot projections) is modeled in Lean 4 (165 catalogued theorems, one cryptographic axiom, 15 audited external assumptions), tied to production Rust by Aeneas extraction of 20 functions with generated equivalence theorems, 41 public Kani harnesses, 9 Creusot contracts, hashed mirrors, and per-PR differential tests, while storage, crypto, clocks, transport, subprocess effects, and settlement remain assumption-bound and runtime-qualified. Around that core sit a recursive swarm-authority verifier (test-qualified, wired through an optional runtime admission hook), a federation layer with revocation gossip, an iroh QUIC transport, governance-ladder treaties and receiver-owned bilateral co-signed DSSE admission (bounded Lean models plus differential and negative-corpus tests, all demonstrated single-host or over loopback), a metering and budget-hold path with a payment-adapter seam and simulated x402 flow, artifact-level markets, credit, and insurance surfaces, web3 anchoring and settlement lanes behind a feature flag with external chain and witness dependencies, an opt-in BBS selective-disclosure slice limited to reveal-set proofs, 28 protocol and provider adapters of which two providers are release-qualified, and receipt verifiers in TypeScript, Python, and Go pinned to a frozen vector corpus.

### Strongest claims that are fully truthful

- Chio has an implementation-linked verified core defined in formal/proof-manifest.toml: the pure admission path (verify_capability, resolve_matching_grants, evaluate, sign_receipt) and its budget, DPoP, nonce, guard-step, revocation-snapshot and receipt-coupling projections are modeled in root-imported Lean 4 with bounded proofs, and 20 production Rust functions are extracted through Charon/Aeneas with generated-to-model equivalence theorems (registry FORM-BOUNDARY, FORM-IMPLEMENTATION-LINKED).
- Every explicit axiom in the root-imported Lean development is a single named cryptographic idealization (Chio.Json.hash_collision_resistant, tied to ASSUME-SHA256); no serializer, protocol, or kernel behavior is axiomatized (registry FORM-NO-SOFTWARE-AXIOMS).
- Delegated capability issuance can only narrow scope: bounded Lean mechanization (delegate_no_widen, scope_subset_of_grants_subset, witness_soundness, sibling_sum_soundness) plus executable three-way differential tests and a Kani harness bound to NormalizedToolGrant::is_subset_of cover capability attenuation over the verified-core model (P1).
- A revoked token or revoked presented ancestor cannot pass the pure revocation/evaluation model (P2, bounded Lean), and the production kernel checks the leaf and every delegation-chain link against its revocation store before dispatch (chio-kernel validation.rs check_revocation, dispatch.rs L700), with runtime revocation-store behavior separately qualified.
- The pure evaluator is total and fail-closed for invalid signature, time window, revocation, and out-of-scope paths (P3, bounded Lean), guard deny and error dominate the pipeline, and the Rust shell's guard, budget, DPoP, revocation and receipt decisions are pinned to the proved projections by equivalence tests.
- Receipt identity is the hash of canonical content recomputed inside the trust boundary before signing (WYSIWYS), canonical JSON injectivity is proved over its modeled domain, and within the published eight-leaf bound the Rust inclusion-proof walk refines the proved Merkle model under ASSUME-SHA256 (P4).
- A receipt signed by the Rust kernel verifies byte-for-byte in TypeScript, Python, and Go against a triple-blessed frozen vector corpus (formal/diff-tests receipt_encoding_diff.rs plus SDK vector tests), and captured evidence packages verify offline with tamper detection (examples/hello-receipt-verify).
- Chio ships a cryptographically signed, fail-closed governance and evidence control plane with recursive delegated-authority admission backed by ancestor capability snapshots, signed local receipts and checkpoints, and explicit local or leader-local operational contracts for trust-control and budgets (the bounded release gate's own strongest wording).
- Receiver-owned treaty checks run before a cross-organization tool call in the runtime admission hook; request metadata cannot install the receiver's trust roots; two distinct configured keys sign the same canonical treaty-bound predicate; a treaty continuation is consumed once and replay denies; all 20 named negative cases return the expected denial (P1 CLAIM_LEDGER supported results, each scoped to the tested construction).
- Distributed revocation has a bounded Apalache model over lossy, duplicating, reordering channels with skew and partitions, a one-origin scalar production projection exercising signer rejection, queueing, view installation, freshness denial and catch-up, and nightly trace validation of RevocationPropagation against real kernel commit, admission and receipt-append traces (registry TRACE-VALIDATED, P2 with distributed_apalache and rust_trace_projection).
- Concurrency and adversarial evidence exists beyond proofs: 10 Loom models, deterministic drop-injection schedules, 28 fuzz targets, mutation campaigns with tracked survivors, and dudect timing harnesses, all with registered negative-test discipline.

### Gaps between VISION.md and the code

- VISION section 4 says Merkle commitment plus signed checkpoint publication is a planned enhancement, while the code ships chio.checkpoint_statement.v2 with chain_root, consistency proofs, equivocation detection and background checkpoints; the accurate statement is neither (checkpoints exist but PROTOCOL 6.5 confines them to audit_only and transparency_preview claims).
- VISION says monetary budgets ship on a Q2 2026 roadmap; the kernel already has monetary caps (max_cost_per_invocation, max_total_cost), durable pre-execution holds, capture and settle paths, and metering receipts.
- VISION section 3 describes an HA leader/follower distributed control plane whose failover preserves invariants; docs/release/QUALIFICATION.md records the bounded release as leader-local trust-control with no consensus-grade HA and no distributed-linearizable budget authority.
- VISION lists seven composable guards; crates/guards/chio-guards ships roughly 30 guard modules (README says 26 named guards) plus data, external and WASM guard families.
- VISION's formal-evidence section omits the treaty and bilateral lane entirely, while README and both papers center it; conversely CURRENT_STATE and README still report 149 catalogued declarations and thirteen assumptions against 165 and 15 in the registries, and Chio.lean imports Treaty/ReceiptPredicate.lean which is absent from proof-manifest root_modules and the theorem inventory.
- VISION's cascade revocation is described as a structural property of the delegation chain; in code it is a runtime store check on the leaf and each chain link plus an optional signed-epoch view, with P2 proved only for the pure model, and the revocation oracle crate is not linked into chio-kernel.
- VISION's reputation network, agent passports as a credit-bureau-like trust layer, certification, underwriting, insurance and the adoption flywheel are strategic; the code has chio-reputation, chio-credentials passports with OID4VCI/OID4VP lanes, chio-underwriting, chio-market insurance artifacts and local workflows, but no adopter data, no cross-issuer scoring (PROTOCOL 14 excludes portable reputation as a universal trust oracle), and QUALIFICATION refuses the market-position thesis.
- VISION and README present recursive swarm delegation as running inside the kernel's admission path; the swarm verifier is invoked only through ChioRuntimeAdmissionHook, which chio-runtime and the runtime harness install and the default chio mcp serve path does not, and the flagship agent_os example never dispatches a call.
- VISION's competitive table lists cross-org trust as delegated capability chains with subject binding; the shipped cross-org construction is treaties plus bilateral DSSE co-signing, demonstrated only single-host or over loopback iroh, with two-key-not-two-org, no separately administered deployment, and a broken artifact reproducibility gate for the paper.
- VISION's guard pipeline as a compliance engine implies threat coverage; docs/security/threat-coverage.md shows 0 of 20 threat rows Covered pending mutation evidence.
- VISION's qualified MCP adapter coverage matches the code, but the README integration matrix marks OpenAI as interception-only and five provider adapters as not release-qualified or governance-deferred, which VISION does not mention.
- VISION and README present BBS+ selective disclosure as traveling with the agent; the crate is an opt-in reveal-set slice with range predicates deferred.
- Anchoring across EVM, Bitcoin and Solana is presented in README as shipped settlement and anchoring; in code it is a web3-feature lane with external chain finality, Rekor inclusion-path verification not implemented, OTS advisory only, and no Rust treaty-attached multi-lane witness quorum (that policy exists only as a Lean model).
- The repository is pre-release (RELEASE_AUDIT decision: continue pre-release evaluation; v1-only surfaces; external release, production and GA claims on hold), which VISION's closing line (the infrastructure is built, the proofs compile, the receipts are signed) does not convey.

## Part B: landscape

### Positioning sentence

Chio is the only agent kernel in which the signed receipt of a tool call is simultaneously the admission evidence a foreign organization's kernel verifies against a signed treaty before dispatch, the unit of account that meters, credits, and insures agents, and the object whose attenuation, revocation, and inclusion semantics are covered by implementation-linked machine-checked proofs; MCP and A2A define the call, x402 and AP2 define the payment, Cedar, OPA, and AuthZEN define the decision, SCITT and Sigstore define the log, and none of them defines the receipt that ties all four to a chain of authority that can only narrow.

### Unclaimed territory

- Receiver-owned, treaty-bound, bilaterally co-signed admission of a foreign organization's agent tool call, verified by the receiving kernel against its own trust store before dispatch (treaty scope and ladder-intersection digests, continuation, lineage, policy-verdict agreement, lease and governance receipts: crates/trust/chio-federation bilateral_verifier and treaty.rs). Every agent-security system found (SAGA, IsolateGPT, OAP, aiAuthZ, CONTINUITY, Sello, ScopeGate, AIP, the 'Delegation Without Trust' broker) protects a local principal; the only cross-domain analogs are IBC light clients and Fabric endorsement, neither of which knows what an agent or a tool call is.
- The receipt as a unit of account: signed allow and deny receipts that carry cost and payer, on which metering (five cost dimensions and a budget tree), credit IOUs minted from finalized receipts, deterministic insurance premium pricing and claims, open-market minting of scoped capability tokens, and x402/EIP-3009/escrow settlement are all built (crates/economy). Payment protocols (x402, AP2, Stripe MPP) prove payment, not authorized action; the 2026 receipt literature (Acta, Asqav, Vaara, Sello, OAP, aiAuthZ) proves action, not payment or economic consequence.
- Attenuation-only delegation with a per-hop signed AttenuationWitness proving the normalized subset relation, basis-point budget splits, caveats, and scope_hash chain binding, checked against a sparse-Merkle revocation oracle with Ed25519-signed epoch roots and Apalache-modelled propagation, and extended to whole swarms by chio-swarm-authority (task-graph, continuation, route-plan, budget-pool, and revocation verification before any child runs). Biscuit, UCAN, ZCAP, and Macaroons have chains without budgets, receipts, or a kernel; AgentCore and Entra have neither attenuation proofs nor cascade revocation.
- Implementation-linked formal evidence governed by a public claim registry: 165 Lean theorems with one audited crypto axiom, Aeneas extraction of 20 kernel-core functions with generated-to-model equivalence theorems, 41 public Kani harnesses, Creusot contracts, Apalache/TLA+ and Loom models, trace validation against real kernel runs, mutation campaigns, and an approved/disallowed-wording table (docs/reference/CLAIM_REGISTRY.md). Cedar verifies a policy evaluator and seL4 verifies a microkernel; nobody has verified attenuation, revocation, DPoP admission, and receipt-inclusion semantics for agent tool calls under a published claim gate.
- A governance ladder for federated agents (observation, guarded, receipt_backed, partition_contingency, maintenance, with declared consistency models and partition fallbacks) plus capability leases and destructive-action governance receipts (crates/trust/chio-governance). The Governance Gaps paper (arXiv 2606.31498, June 30, 2026) concludes that MCP, A2A, ACP, ANP, and ERC-8004 cannot express membership, deliberation, voting, dissent preservation, human escalation, or audit/replay and that a governance layer above interoperability standards is missing; Chio partially occupies membership, human escalation, and audit/replay, and nobody occupies the rest.
- A cross-protocol evidence graph: chio-agent-web-interop verifies transaction passports that bind evidence from 30 external protocols (MCP, A2A, ACP-Client, ACP-Commerce, AG-UI, AP2, x402, OAuth2/OIDC, SCIM, SPIFFE, Kubernetes admission, Sigstore, SLSA, in-toto, DSSE, OCI, VC, SD-JWT VC, BBS, webhooks, CloudEvents, GraphQL, OpenAPI, AsyncAPI, and SaaS APIs) under an authority taxonomy in which external proofs never become Chio authority. No vendor or standard offers a verifier that composes another protocol's evidence with a signed admission receipt.

### Threats

- AWS: AgentCore Gateway plus Policy (Cedar, default-deny per-tool-call with argument conditions) plus Identity already occupies the local half of Chio's story with a Lean-verified policy language; adding signed decision receipts, SCITT-style registration, and cross-account verification through Verified Permissions and CloudTrail Lake would cover most of the pitch for AWS customers within one or two release cycles and blunt the formal-evidence differentiator.
- Microsoft: Entra Agent ID (GA April 2026), the stable MCP Enterprise-Managed Authorization extension, Azure confidential computing, and Microsoft's own authorship of the CCF profile for COSE Receipts and SCITT give it every piece needed to ship 'signed agent action receipts registered to a transparency service' as a product before any paper is accepted.
- Google and the Agentic AI Foundation: A2A, AP2 mandates (verifiable credentials), and the A2A x402 extension now sit under one foundation with 150+ organizations; a signed task-completion receipt in A2A v1.x bound to a mandate would occupy the payment-bound action receipt space, and Chio's A2A adapter (v1.0.0 fixtures) would be a follower.
- Coinbase, Cloudflare, and ERC-8004: the x402 Foundation (22 members including AWS, Google, Microsoft, Stripe, Visa, Mastercard) plus deployed ERC-8004 identity, reputation, and validation registries provide public, portable, on-chain per-agent evidence and stablecoin settlement; a validation-registry entry per agent action is a public receipt that Chio's operator-local, optionally anchored log is not.
- Enterprise IAM (Okta/Auth0 XAA with ID-JAG, OpenID AuthZEN COAZ profile for MCP tool authorization and AARP approvals, Nirmata AIControls already 'policy-checks, budgets, and attributes every delegated MCP and LLM call'): IETF and OpenID documents can standardize identity-bound, budgeted, approval-gated per-call MCP authorization that enterprises will prefer over a Chio-native profile, while Chio's own OAuth authorization-server surface is feature-gated and blocked pending an ADR.
- IETF composition: SCITT architecture, COSE Receipts, ID-JAG, WIMSE, and the individual signed-action-receipt drafts (draft-farley-acta-signed-receipts-00 with an in-toto predicate type, draft-marques-asqav-compliance-receipts-08 with EU AI Act, DORA, HIPAA, and SEC 17a-4 bindings, draft-sirkkavaara-vaara-receipt-07) could converge on a standards-track agent action receipt with regulatory bindings; Chio's canonical-JSON receipt and its bilateral predicate remain unregistered proposals.
- Academic near-misses that could publish first on the receiver-side or formal angle: Sello/Notarized Agents (arXiv 2606.04193, receiver-signed HPKE-encrypted receipts on a witness-cosigned Merkle log, which inverts the trust boundary the way Chio's receiver-owned admission does), CONTINUITY (arXiv 2609.05269, signed root grants, role-bound transition receipts, effect-bound execution permits, formalized consequence integrity, public code), AIP (arXiv 2603.24775, Biscuit-chained invocation-bound capability tokens across MCP and A2A), Open Agent Passport (arXiv 2603.20953, Apache-2.0 spec with signed audit records), ScopeGate (arXiv 2606.28679), and the 'Delegation Without Trust' authorization broker (arXiv 2609.00267). P1's related work cites only IsolateGPT, SAGA, and the agentic-foundations paper; a USENIX or NDSS reviewer will expect these.
- Anthropic and OpenAI: Claude Managed Agents and Codex cloud already ship OS sandboxes, scoped permissions, and session tracing; if either adds a signed, exportable tool-call ledger with delegable scopes, the receipt becomes a platform feature and Chio's provider adapters become the integration point rather than the authority.
- Transparency-infrastructure drift: Rekor v2 removed the intoto entry type that Chio's RekorClient posts to the v1 API and moved to tile-based logs, and Chio's anchor WitnessPolicy is single-witness; a stale public-witness lane weakens the anchoring claim exactly where reviewers will probe.
- Sandbox vendors moving up-stack (Cloudflare Dynamic Workers and Sandbox SDK, E2B, Modal, Daytona): adding a policy-and-receipt layer above hardware isolation is easier than Chio adding hardware isolation, since chio-cage (Landlock plus seccomp-BPF) is an unshipped design and ASSUME-SUBPROCESS-ISOLATION delegates tool-effect containment to the host; 'isolation plus receipts' could be claimed by them first.
- Ledger and interchain communities: Fabric-X and IBC Eureka are expanding aggressively; an 'agent endorsement policy' chaincode or an IBC packet type for agent tool calls would deliver multi-organization co-signing with consensus and light-client verification, exceeding Chio's two-signature bilateral verifier and pinned-key trust.
- Own goals visible at HEAD that hand the territory to others: the broken artifact reproducibility gate, benchmark and environment inconsistencies, P2 describing a wire format and rejection taxonomy that do not exist, P1 naming 'quorum-required' as a ladder mode when the code ranks 'maintenance', and repository docs that overclaim (Rego guards that do not exist). The formal and evidence differentiator is only as strong as the claim discipline, and reviewers can check every one of these in minutes.

### Systems and standards

#### MCP (Model Context Protocol) and its authorization spec

- What it does: JSON-RPC tool/resource/prompt protocol between LLM hosts and servers over stdio or streamable HTTP. Authorization spec is an OAuth 2.1 subset: the MCP server is an OAuth resource server; RFC 9728 protected-resource metadata is MUST; RFC 8707 resource parameter is MUST in both authorization and token requests; RFC 9207 iss validation; Client ID Metadata Documents are SHOULD and Dynamic Client Registration is deprecated; scope challenges and step-up flow; stdio transport is explicitly out of scope. Extension repo (ext-auth) lists Enterprise-Managed Authorization (stable) and Client Credentials (draft). Secondary sources date the current revision 2026-07-28 and say EMA builds on ID-JAG; that detail was not verified against the primary text.
- Overlap with Chio: Chio wraps and hosts MCP: crates/protocol/chio-mcp-adapter (AdaptedMcpServer keeps MCP annotations such as destructiveHint), chio-mcp-edge, chio-mcp-remote, chio-hosted-mcp; the hosted edge speaks exactly MCP 2025-11-25 (spec/ietf/draft-chio-protocol-00.md section 3.2) and publishes /.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/mcp; scripts/check-adapter-no-bypass.sh is a static gate that MCP mediation cannot bypass the kernel. Chio's IETF-style draft positions its hosted edge as MCP-compatible.
- Chio has, it lacks: Per-call capability verification with argument constraints (ToolGrant/Constraint in capability/scope.rs), attenuation-only delegation with signed per-hop proofs, invocation and monetary budgets held before dispatch, signed allow and deny receipts in an RFC 6962 Merkle log, a Merkle revocation oracle, sender constraints (DPoP per grant), and cross-organization treaty admission. MCP authorization stops at bearer-token audience binding and scope strings; it has no receipts, no delegation semantics, and DPoP is not normative.
- It has, Chio lacks: Ecosystem scale and Agentic AI Foundation governance; a standardized OAuth discovery/registration flow that every IdP implements; the Enterprise-Managed Authorization extension; elicitation, sampling, and tasks features. Chio's hosted edge pins one protocol version (2025-11-25, older than the July 2026 revision reported by secondary sources) and its own OAuth authorization-server profile (chio-governed-rar-v1) is feature-gated and 'blocked for product use until a dedicated OAuth AS ADR' (spec/PROTOCOL.md near line 2711).
- Reference: https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/draft/basic/authorization ; https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/ext-auth

#### Google A2A (Agent2Agent)

- What it does: Vendor-neutral agent-to-agent task protocol over JSON-RPC 2.0 / HTTP+JSON with Agent Cards for discovery. v1.0 (March 12, 2026 per secondary sources) formalized JWS-signed Agent Cards (RFC 7515 with JCS RFC 8785), multitenancy, and protocol negotiation. Donated to the Linux Foundation in 2025 and moved under the Agentic AI Foundation in August 2026 (Axios); 150+ supporting organizations; an A2A x402 extension exists.
- Overlap with Chio: crates/protocol/chio-a2a-adapter discovers Agent Cards, projects skills into a signed ToolManifest, resolves per-skill security schemes (bearer, OAuth2 client-credentials, OIDC, API key, mTLS), and routes SendMessage plus six task-lifecycle calls through the kernel under a typed HttpEgressContract; chio-a2a-edge exposes Chio tools as A2A skills; chio-agent-web-interop binds A2A v1.0.0 task evidence as external proof (docs/standards/CHIO_AGENT_WEB_PROTOCOL_TAXONOMY.md: 'A2A alone is not Chio authority').
- Chio has, it lacks: Scoped, attenuable authority per task, signed receipts of what was authorized and what happened, budgets, cascade revocation, and treaty-bound cross-org admission with policy-verdict agreement. A2A relies on transport auth schemes declared in cards; it has no attenuation, no receipts, and no cross-org policy verdict.
- It has, Chio lacks: Adoption and foundation governance, streaming and push-notification task semantics, a standardized discovery ecosystem. Chio has no discovery network of its own and treats A2A strictly as evidence.
- Reference: https://opensource.googleblog.com/2026/04/a-year-of-open-collaboration-celebrating-the-anniversary-of-a2a.html ; https://www.axios.com/2026/08/17/a2a-agentic-ai-foundation-open-ai-standards

#### AP2 (Agent Payments Protocol)

- What it does: Google's agent payment protocol (announced September 2025) built as an A2A extension: Intent Mandates, Cart Mandates, and Payment Mandates are cryptographically signed verifiable credentials that encode user payment instructions and signal agent involvement to networks. The A2A x402 extension (with Coinbase and MetaMask) is described as production-ready for crypto payments as of April 2026 (secondary sources); card-rail implementations are still maturing.
- Overlap with Chio: Chio's GovernedTransactionIntent / GovernedApprovalToken (crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/governance.rs) and chio-commerce-order model approval-bound intent hashes; chio-agent-web-interop binds AP2 mandate evidence and cross-checks a bound CommerceOrderContext; the taxonomy classifies AP2 as commerce evidence that must be bound to the order context.
- Chio has, it lacks: The link between the mandate and the tool invocations that led to it: Chio receipts record capability id, action, cost, payer, and the approval-bound intent hash, and the budget hold is taken before the call and sealed into the receipt; spend authority attenuates through the delegation chain (bps budget share); credit and insurance are built on those receipts.
- It has, Chio lacks: A merchant/PSP/card-network coalition, consumer consent UX, and a VC-based mandate format wallets and networks accept. Chio has no card-network integration, and VC Data Integrity interop is explicitly deferred (spec/CHIO_SELECTIVE_DISCLOSURE.md section 2.3).
- Reference: https://ap2-protocol.org/ ; https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-agents-to-payments-ap2-protocol

#### x402

- What it does: HTTP 402 stablecoin micropayment protocol: server returns 402 with payment requirements, client retries with a signed payment header settled by a facilitator (Base and Solana dominant). x402 Foundation announced by Coinbase and Cloudflare September 23, 2025 and formalized under the Linux Foundation April 2, 2026 with 22 members including AWS, Google, Microsoft, Stripe, Visa, Mastercard; the V2 spec is the baseline; Coinbase reported 69k active agents and 165M transactions by late April 2026 (secondary).
- Overlap with Chio: crates/economy/chio-settle/src/payments.rs defines X402PaymentRequirements (settlement_mode PrepaidAuthorization | EscrowBacked, governed_authorization_required, capability_id, dispatch_id), EIP-3009 transferWithAuthorization digests, Circle nanopayments, and ERC-4337 paymaster checks; crates/kernel/chio-kernel/src/payment.rs defines the PaymentAdapter (authorize / capture / release / refund, idempotent by reference) invoked around dispatch; escrow and bond contracts bind to capability ids and anchor inclusion proofs.
- Chio has, it lacks: Authorization of the tool call itself, a pre-call budget hold sealed into a signed receipt, payer attribution in the receipt, delegation-attenuated spend limits, and escrow/bond settlement tied to receipts. x402 proves payment, not that the paid-for action was authorized or what it did.
- It has, Chio lacks: A live settlement network, facilitators, and a foundation with payment-industry members; on-chain receipts are public and portable whereas Chio receipts are operator-local unless anchored. Chio's x402 surface prepares compatibility artifacts; it is not a facilitator.
- Reference: https://www.coinbase.com/blog/coinbase-and-cloudflare-will-launch-x402-foundation ; https://www.x402.org/

#### SAGA (Security Architecture for Governing AI Agentic Systems)

- What it does: Syros, Suri, Ginesin, Nita-Rotaru, Oprea. A centralized Provider registers agents and user-defined access policies; users retain lifecycle control; a cryptographic mechanism derives one-time access-control tokens that gate agent-to-agent contact with formal security guarantees and low overhead across on-device and cloud LLMs. arXiv 2504.21034 (April 2025). Venue correction: it appears at NDSS 2026 (33rd), not NDSS 2025; P1's bib already records 2026 and the NDSS PDF is filed as 2026-s869.
- Overlap with Chio: User-controlled scoping of who may contact an agent resembles Chio capability issuance plus the A2A partner policy; both are fail-closed token designs with a formal model of token derivation.
- Chio has, it lacks: Tool-level scopes with argument constraints, attenuation proofs, budgets, signed per-decision receipts and a Merkle log, a revocation oracle, cross-org treaties, and economic primitives. SAGA governs contact between agents, not the actions an agent takes on tools.
- It has, Chio lacks: A single-Provider, user-centric lifecycle model that is simpler to deploy, a compact formal treatment of token derivation, and a public artifact (github.com/gsiros/saga).
- Reference: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21034 ; https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2026-s869-paper.pdf

#### IsolateGPT

- What it does: Wu, Roesner, Kohno, Zhang, Iqbal; NDSS 2025 (32nd), arXiv 2403.04960. Hub-and-spoke execution isolation for LLM app ecosystems: each third-party app runs in its own spoke with its own LLM instance; the hub mediates app selection and inter-spoke communication with user permission; overhead under 30% for three-quarters of tested queries.
- Overlap with Chio: Privilege separation with a trusted mediator: Chio's kernel is the hub analog and tool servers are isolated from the agent and each other (AGENTS.md five components); WASM guards run fuel-metered under wasmtime.
- Chio has, it lacks: Cryptographic capability tokens and signed receipts (IsolateGPT permissions are runtime user prompts, unsigned and non-portable), cascade revocation, cross-org admission, formal proofs, and economic primitives.
- It has, Chio lacks: Isolation of the LLM reasoning itself per app (separate LLM contexts and natural-language information-flow gates). Chio treats the agent as one untrusted process and does not partition model context.
- Reference: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04960 ; https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/isolategpt-an-execution-isolation-architecture-for-llm-based-agentic-systems/

#### Cedar

- What it does: AWS authorization policy language: permit/forbid with default-deny and forbid-overrides, schema-validated; authorizer and validator are modeled and proved in Lean with differential random testing (OOPSLA 2024); Cedar Analysis provides SMT-backed policy analysis; Cedar is the engine behind Amazon Verified Permissions and Bedrock AgentCore Policy (GA March 2026 per secondary sources).
- Overlap with Chio: crates/guards/chio-policy is Chio's native HushSpec YAML policy engine with a bounded static analyzer (rule shadowing, unreachable rules, refinement with widening witnesses) that docs/formal/plan/FV-C4-policy-smt-analyzer.md names Zelkova and Cedar's analyzer as prior art; formal/lean4 Treaty/PredicateLang.lean is a Cedar-style syntactic Predicate ADT with decidable refinesOn; both projects verify in Lean.
- Chio has, it lacks: Policies bound to signed, caller-held capabilities and to signed receipts; attenuation across delegation; revocation; budgets; treaties. Cedar is a stateless decision function with no token or receipt model.
- It has, Chio lacks: A general-purpose, production-verified policy language with schema typing and SMT analysis, adopted across AWS products. Chio ships no Cedar evaluator or interop (no cedar dependency in any Cargo.toml) and HushSpec's analyzer is bounded to a decidable fragment (POLICY-ANALYZE claim).
- Reference: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3649835 ; https://lean-lang.org/use-cases/cedar/

#### OPA / Rego

- What it does: CNCF-graduated general-purpose policy engine; OPA 1.0 (2025) made Rego v1 syntax the default; ubiquitous as Envoy ext_authz PDP, Kubernetes admission (Gatekeeper), CI gates; policies compile to WASM.
- Overlap with Chio: crates/protocol/chio-envoy-ext-authz implements envoy.service.auth.v3.Authorization/Check so Chio can stand where OPA stands; sdks/k8s ships admission webhooks that verify capabilities fail-closed; docs/protocols/ENVOY-EXT-AUTHZ-INTEGRATION.md describes migrating from OPA to Chio.
- Chio has, it lacks: Signed capability tokens, signed receipts, budgets, revocation cascade, formal proofs; OPA decisions are unsigned, stateless, and carry no delegation semantics.
- It has, Chio lacks: A general policy language with a data-document model and an enormous ecosystem. Verified at HEAD: docs/protocols/ENVOY-EXT-AUTHZ-INTEGRATION.md (lines 406, 628, 990) claims 'Rego guards run OPA policies natively', but no Rego evaluator exists: no regorus/opa dependency in any Cargo.toml and no word-bounded 'rego' identifier in any .rs file. That doc overclaims and should not be cited in the paper.
- Reference: https://www.openpolicyagent.org/blog/announcing-opa-1-0-a-new-standard-for-policy-as-code-a6d8427ee828

#### Zanzibar (and OpenFGA / SpiceDB)

- What it does: Google's global relationship-based access control (USENIX ATC 2019): relation tuples, userset rewrite rules, zookies for snapshot consistency (the new-enemy problem). Open implementations: OpenFGA (CNCF incubating since October 2025) and SpiceDB (closest fidelity to the paper).
- Overlap with Chio: Minimal. Both answer 'may S do A on R'; Chio's finding-market hosted edge has principal roles and per-tenant row-level security, but authorization is capability-based, not relationship-based.
- Chio has, it lacks: Caller-held, attenuable capabilities verifiable without a central relationship store; signed receipts; budgets; cross-org treaties; formal proofs.
- It has, Chio lacks: ReBAC expressiveness (groups, hierarchies, inheritance), snapshot-consistent checks at planet scale, and mature open-source engines. Chio has no relationship graph or ReBAC model at all.
- Reference: https://research.google/pubs/zanzibar-googles-consistent-global-authorization-system/ ; https://openfga.dev/

#### OpenID AuthZEN

- What it does: Authorization API 1.0 became an OpenID Final Specification on January 12, 2026: a PEP-to-PDP JSON API (subject, action, resource, context) with evaluation, evaluations, and search endpoints. On June 15, 2026 the WG approved two drafts: the Access Request and Approval Profile (AARP) for prerequisite approvals/consent/attestations before a decision, and COAZ, a profile for MCP tool authorization that maps tool calls into the subject-action-resource-context model for gateways and meshes.
- Overlap with Chio: Chio's Guard trait (crates/kernel/chio-kernel-core/src/guard.rs), the EnvoyKernel seam, the Go sidecar Evaluate call, and the K8s webhooks are PEP/PDP splits; AARP's prerequisite approvals map to Chio's GovernedApprovalToken, ThresholdApprovalProposal, and pending elicitation state.
- Chio has, it lacks: Signed decisions (receipts) rather than unsigned JSON verdicts, caller-held capabilities, budgets, and cross-org bilateral verdict agreement (the policy.verdict_disagreement rejection code).
- It has, Chio lacks: A vendor-neutral, final standard API that IdPs, API gateways, and service meshes implement, with an MCP-specific profile in progress. Chio exposes no AuthZEN endpoint; the term does not appear in crates or docs.
- Reference: https://openid.net/specs/authorization-api-1_0.html ; https://openid.net/openid-foundation-advances-authorization-for-the-agent-era-with-new-authzen-working-group-drafts/

#### Macaroons

- What it does: Birgisson et al., NDSS 2014: HMAC-chained bearer tokens whose holder can attenuate by appending first-party caveats (predicates) or third-party caveats discharged by external services; verification requires the root secret key.
- Overlap with Chio: Chio caveats (crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/caveat.rs: Caveat, CaveatKind, GrantSubsetRelation) and attenuation-only delegation are the same discipline; a Chio DelegationLink chain is the public-key analog of a caveat chain.
- Chio has, it lacks: Public-key verification (Ed25519 links, no shared secret), an explicit AttenuationWitness proving the normalized subset relation between parent and child scope, basis-point budget splits, scope_hash chain binding against parent-scope inflation, a revocation oracle, signed receipts, and bounded Lean proofs (P1, P5).
- It has, Chio lacks: Extremely cheap HMAC verification and third-party caveats discharged by arbitrary external services. Chio has no discharge-macaroon analog; external approvals are separate signed approval tokens.
- Reference: https://research.google/pubs/macaroons-cookies-with-contextual-caveats-for-decentralized-authorization-in-the-cloud/

#### Biscuit

- What it does: Eclipse Biscuit: public-key bearer tokens with offline attenuation (append a block signed with a fresh key) and a Datalog authorization language embedded in the token; verifiable by anyone holding the root public key; v3 specification current (a v4 was not verified).
- Overlap with Chio: Signed attenuation chains; Chio's DelegationLink / AttenuationProof design is the structural cousin. AIP (arXiv 2603.24775, March 2026) already uses Biscuit-chained invocation-bound capability tokens across MCP and A2A.
- Chio has, it lacks: A kernel TCB that dispatches and signs receipts, invocation and monetary budgets in the token, a sparse-Merkle revocation oracle with signed epoch roots, treaties, and economics.
- It has, Chio lacks: A logic-programming policy language inside the token and fully offline verification. Chio scopes are structured grants, and chain validation requires persisted ancestor capability snapshots (validate_delegation_chain_with_trust_root), i.e. locally trusted lineage rather than pure offline verification.
- Reference: https://www.biscuitsec.org/ ; https://github.com/eclipse-biscuit/biscuit/blob/master/SPECIFICATIONS.md

#### UCAN (User Controlled Authorization Networks)

- What it does: DID-based capability tokens with monotonic attenuation; the 1.0 spec family covers delegation, invocation, receipts (executor-signed descriptions of output and effects, no expiry), and revocation (revoking a UCAN by CID MUST invalidate all derivatives).
- Overlap with Chio: Closest token-model analog; docs/reference/COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md (dated 2026-03-21) already compares. Chio delegation chains, receipts, and cascade revocation cover the same four concerns.
- Chio has, it lacks: A kernel separated from the resource server, mandatory receipts on allow and deny, budgets inside the token, an RFC 6962 receipt log with checkpoints and anchoring, Lean proofs, and cross-org treaties; Ed25519 over RFC 8785 canonical JSON instead of JWT/IPLD.
- It has, Chio lacks: DID-native, IPLD/CID content addressing, a community spec with TypeScript/Rust/Go implementations and receipt 'effects' semantics. Chio has no UCAN interop.
- Reference: https://github.com/ucan-wg/spec ; https://ucan.xyz/revocation/

#### ZCAP-LD (Authorization Capabilities for Linked Data)

- What it does: W3C CCG work item, v0.4.0 editor's draft: JSON-LD capability documents chained by Data Integrity proofs, with caveats and invocation proofs; still a draft with active issues opened as late as August 2026.
- Overlap with Chio: A delegation chain of signed capability documents with DID controllers, the same shape as Chio's DelegationLink chain.
- Chio has, it lacks: A production runtime, budgets, receipts, a revocation oracle, formal proofs, treaties. ZCAP is a data model without an enforcement kernel.
- It has, Chio lacks: Linked-Data/DID ecosystem alignment and Data Integrity proofs. Chio remains did:chio-first and does not claim generic VC or Data Integrity neutrality (docs/standards/CHIO_PROTOCOL_ALIGNMENT_MATRIX.md).
- Reference: https://w3c-ccg.github.io/zcap-spec/

#### W3C Verifiable Credentials / Presentations

- What it does: VC Data Model 2.0 and six companion specs (Data Integrity, JOSE/COSE securing, etc.) became W3C Recommendations on May 15, 2025; presentations let holders prove claims; OID4VCI/OID4VP transports; SD-JWT VC; EUDI wallet alignment.
- Overlap with Chio: crates/trust/chio-credentials implements the Agent Passport (Ed25519 reputation credentials under did:chio issuer/subject) with lossy projections into OID4VCI, OID4VP, SD-JWT VC, and JWT VC JSON; crates/trust/chio-did; crates/trust/chio-selective-disclosure adds BBS (bbs-2023 pinned) reveal-set proofs over receipts; AP2 mandates are VCs that chio-agent-web-interop can bind as evidence.
- Chio has, it lacks: Credentials derived from signed receipts and reputation scorecards (evidence-backed rather than issuer-asserted), plus the kernel, budgets, and receipts themselves.
- It has, Chio lacks: A standards-track data model with a broad wallet/issuer/verifier ecosystem. Chio's OID4VC lanes are bounded profiles, VC Data Integrity interop and hidden-predicate proofs are deferred, and the EUDI path needs a separate SD-JWT VC mapping (spec/CHIO_SELECTIVE_DISCLOSURE.md sections 2.3 and 14).
- Reference: https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/

#### SPIFFE / SPIRE

- What it does: CNCF-graduated workload identity: SPIFFE IDs, X.509 and JWT SVIDs issued from node/workload attestation, automatic rotation, trust-domain federation. IETF WIMSE architecture and workload-identity BCP drafts extend it, and agent-identity drafts build on it.
- Overlap with Chio: crates/core/chio-core-types/src/capability/workload_identity.rs normalizes SPIFFE URIs (credential kinds uri, x509_svid, jwt_svid) from runtime attestation evidence for policy, governed validation, and receipt metadata (spec/PROTOCOL.md near line 2843); chio-agent-web-interop has a SPIFFE artifact validator; chio-envoy-ext-authz extracts mTLS principals.
- Chio has, it lacks: Authority (what the workload may do, attenuated per hop), receipts, budgets. The taxonomy says it plainly: 'SPIFFE identifies workloads. It does not delegate agent authority.'
- It has, Chio lacks: Production attestation-driven identity issuance and rotation at scale. Chio has no SPIRE agent/server integration: no SVID issuance, no workload attestation plugin, no mTLS via SPIRE (docs/reference/COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md section 4).
- Reference: https://spiffe.io/ ; https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-wimse-arch/

#### OAuth Token Exchange (RFC 8693) and Rich Authorization Requests (RFC 9396)

- What it does: RFC 8693 (January 2020) exchanges tokens for delegated or impersonated tokens with actor claims; RFC 9396 (May 2023) adds typed authorization_details for fine-grained requests. The Identity Assertion JWT Authorization Grant (draft-ietf-oauth-identity-assertion-authz-grant-04) applies them to agents and is shipping as Okta Cross App Access (rolling out August 2026) and Auth0.
- Overlap with Chio: spec/PROTOCOL.md defines profile chio-governed-rar-v1 (schema chio.oauth.authorization-profile.v1) with authorization_details families chio_governed_tool, chio_governed_commerce, chio_governed_metered_billing and a chio_transaction_context block; sender constraints projected into cnf (chioSenderKey, x5t#S256, chioAttestationSha256); governed receipts project into OAuth authorization-context reports; the Go SDK exercises a discover/resolve/exchange OAuth flow (sdks/go/chio-go/auth/oauth_test.go).
- Chio has, it lacks: Per-hop signed attenuation proofs (token exchange narrows by convention, not proof), receipts, budgets, and cascade revocation (revoking a refresh token does not invalidate exchanged descendants).
- It has, Chio lacks: Standardized enterprise IdP integration that every vendor implements. Chio's OAuth authorization-server surface is feature-gated and explicitly 'blocked for product use until a dedicated OAuth AS ADR or equivalent decision note is accepted'.
- Reference: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8693 ; https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9396 ; https://oauth.net/cross-app-access/

#### DPoP (RFC 9449)

- What it does: September 2023: sender-constrained OAuth access tokens via a per-request signed JWT proof (htm, htu, jti, iat, nonce) bound to the token through cnf.jkt, defeating replay of stolen bearer tokens.
- Overlap with Chio: crates/kernel/chio-kernel/src/dpop.rs (DpopProofBody, DpopProof::sign, DpopNonceStore, verify_dpop_proof with freshness and nonce checks), capability_request_requires_dpop per grant (request_matching.rs), and pure helpers dpop_admits / dpop_freshness_valid in chio-kernel-core formal_core.rs and formal_aeneas.rs covered by the FORM-IMPLEMENTATION-LINKED claim; the hosted OAuth path enforces DPoP at token exchange and again at protected-resource admission.
- Chio has, it lacks: Proof-of-possession tied to a specific capability grant and recorded in the receipt, with the admission helper extracted to Lean.
- It has, Chio lacks: RFC wire compatibility across IdPs. The alignment matrix states Chio 'also carries a Chio-native DPoP format and does not claim the native framed lane is RFC 9449 wire-compatible', and not every capability is sender-constrained (COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md 7.5).
- Reference: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9449

#### DSSE (Dead Simple Signing Envelope)

- What it does: v1.0 envelope (secure-systems-lab): PAE(payloadType, payload) signed by one or more keys; the substrate for in-toto, SLSA provenance, and Sigstore attestations.
- Overlap with Chio: Chio's bilateral co-sign artifact is a DSSE envelope with exactly two signatures over an in-toto-shaped Statement whose predicate type is chio.bilateral-cosign-invocation.v1 (crates/trust/chio-federation/src/bilateral_dsse/verify.rs); the Rekor witness lane publishes DSSE.
- Chio has, it lacks: A receiver-side verification algorithm (17 steps in spec/CHIO_BILATERAL_COSIGN_INVOCATION.md) binding predicate to treaty scope, ladder intersection, continuation, lineage, receipts, leases, and policy verdicts, plus a signer-independence requirement. DSSE is a substrate, not a competitor. Note for the papers: at HEAD the envelope verifier checks all structural gates first and verifies the two Ed25519 signatures last, contradicting P2's ordering claim.
- It has, Chio lacks: Nothing beyond the envelope itself; ubiquity.
- Reference: https://github.com/secure-systems-lab/dsse

#### in-toto attestation framework

#### Sigstore / Rekor
