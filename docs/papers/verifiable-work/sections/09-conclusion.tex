\section{Conclusion}
Chio provides a kernel architecture for programs whose work develops across
independently controlled resources. Agents determine what to attempt next.
Receiving kernels determine what may execute, retain its history and issue
evidence under their owners' authority. Capabilities and treaties give this
cooperation explicit terms; work commitments bind those terms to particular
invocations and their resources.

The construction preserves these bindings as the task graph grows. A later
plan cannot renew a consumed execution right, recovery consults the original
execution record, and an earned payment retains its separately funded reserve.
Under the stated assumptions, these rules preserve resource bounds, invocation
identity and earned claims through the specified transitions.

This is the execution model Chio offers the agentic web. A program can discover
tasks and select collaborators during execution while each participant retains
control over admission to its domain. Planning can be distributed and revised;
the commitments governing its effects remain in the custody of the authorities
responsible for enforcing them.
