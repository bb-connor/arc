\section{Where the argument stops}
D1 has a trusted owner allocator and one protected custody domain per receiver
key. It cannot prevent those trusted components from equivocating after state
fork or rollback. Its offline permits require prior qualification and do not
provide instantaneous global revocation. Sealing may strand unused capacity;
expired, withheld or unresolved work has no automatic reclaim path. Output
validation and recovery recheck contract expiry, so completion after that
deadline may remain stranded. The
implemented predicates and finite trees intentionally restrict expressiveness.
Declared reader/effect attenuation still depends on complete host mediation.
The profile does not track arbitrary information flow through tools, logs,
errors and payment channels.

S1 conservatively retains every allocation and prior permission. Its storage
and verification costs grow with the retained graph and history; no scalability
or hostile-load result is reported. Policy changes, revocation-epoch replacement,
task retirement, budget reclaim and receiver migration require explicit
lifecycle rules beyond additive extension. The composed adapter now binds
D1 and S1 allocations to F1 escrow through the exact funded request. It still
has one protected graph writer and author-controlled receiver replicas. It does
not establish consensus, atomic cross-owner installation or receiver migration.
Its execution statements attest receiver-local observations; successful payment
does not imply that a final bilateral receipt reached the other party.

The two enduring dependencies in F1 are the acceptance authority and the
settlement domain. Parties may choose them without choosing a shared task
scheduler, but cannot dispense with their assumptions. An escrow administrator
can restrict new participation; an asset issuer may freeze transfers; a
verifier may censor or falsely approve. Immutable earned-state rules alone
do not defeat those failures. No new token, global reputation system or
universal identity registry is necessary for the demonstrated profile.

Verifiable work means work with an agreed check, not universally decidable
quality. A test suite may miss a vulnerability. A proof may prove the wrong
specification. A correct declared-authentication inventory may have little
commercial value. The buyer must choose an acceptance procedure fit for its
purpose and a disclosure policy it can tolerate. Once disclosed, data cannot
be recalled by revoking a capability.

Evidence integrity is narrower than availability. A content hash detects
substitution but cannot recover withheld bytes. A custody signature records a
promise to retain data; it does not prove future service or erase storage costs.
Likewise, a historical signed receipt can remain valid after authority expires,
while fresh dispatch must still consult current authority and revocation state.

Our safety argument abstracts serial atomic rail transitions, and the local
experiments use a private development chain or an owned chain fixture. They do
not establish public-chain finality, independent observers, geographic failure
tolerance, sustained denial-of-service resilience or hostile administration of
the honest party's kernel. The Lean model and finite exploration have explicit
boundaries; passing them does not prove the implementation as a whole.

The next decisive evaluation is an independently implemented provider and
separately administered participants completing useful held-out work from the
published contract. It must allow the alternative the same escrow, checker,
models, budgets and ordinary application extensions. It should measure
integration changes, failed-attempt cost, capital duration, verifier cost,
recovery effort and buyer usefulness. Without those results, neither lower
integration cost nor a profitable autonomous supply chain is an empirical
conclusion of this paper.
