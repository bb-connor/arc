\section{Scope}
\label{sec:scope}
The preservation result applies to the composition rules specified in this
paper. Plan extension is additive: old tasks, allocations, permissions and
continuation identifiers remain in place. Removing tasks, reclaiming their budgets, migrating
receivers or changing the graph's policy limits would require closure or fencing
rules beyond this operation. A timeout cannot justify reclaiming a resource
whose use is still uncertain. Retaining earlier graph versions also incurs
storage and verification costs.

Acceptance is as strong as the agreed check. The implemented delegation
predicates test bounded properties of JSON output; the funded verifier can
apply a richer procedure. Success of a child does not establish success of its
parent. The host must mediate declared operations and readers, and revocation
cannot recall information already disclosed.

The safety result permits unavailable or unfinished work. Sealing can strand
capacity; contract expiry can prevent output or recovery checks from completing.
Hashes detect altered evidence but cannot retrieve withheld evidence. An earned
claim preserves the right to withdraw, while collection still requires an
available settlement system and an eligible beneficiary. Verifier failure before
acceptance is recorded can leave completed work unpaid.

Deployment must protect the authorities in Section~\ref{sec:sovereignty}.
A compromised allocator can overissue; forked execution stores can duplicate
authority; a dishonest verifier can approve bad output. The asset and settlement
observation rule must support the claimed financial transitions. The local
experiments establish the reported behavior under their stated configuration.
They do not measure independent administration, public-chain finality,
hostile-load scalability, integration savings or useful-work profitability.
