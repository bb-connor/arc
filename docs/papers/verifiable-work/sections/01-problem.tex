\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
An agent program can acquire tasks and collaborators as it runs. It may inspect
data held by one organization, delegate analysis to another and purchase a
service from a third. Its control flow develops through these interactions,
while the resources it uses remain subject to different owners. Executing such
a program requires records establishing what each participant may do, what it
has already done and which obligations its work has created.

The architectural problem is to preserve the meaning of those records as the
plan changes. Delegation of a task, authorization to execute it, evidence of its
outcome and acceptance for payment are distinct decisions. A new plan must
respect resources already committed. A replacement process must recover the
history of earlier actions. An owner must be able to enforce its own conditions
when admitting work from another participant.

We present Chio, a kernel architecture for peer-to-peer verifiable work. Chio
separates agent planning from the authority and durable state governing its
effects. An untrusted agent proposes operations. A receiving kernel admits
them under its owner's capabilities, policies and treaties, assigns their
execution rights to durable records, and mediates access to local tools.
Signed receipts make the kernel's decisions available as evidence to other
participants. Each participant selects the authorities whose evidence it accepts.

The unit of composition is a \emph{work commitment}: linked signed records that
bind a selected invocation to a bounded allowance, the receiving owner's
authorization and, for paid work, its settlement terms. The allocator, receiver
and settlement system each retain the state for the resource they control.
Authenticated references connect their decisions to the same invocation.
The resulting program spans these authorities without requiring one of them
to control every participant's resources.

This organization gives programmable sovereignty an operational meaning.
Owners express conditions for cooperation through capabilities, policies and
treaties, and enforce those conditions at admission. A foreign agreement is
evidence evaluated under that local authority. Once work has been admitted,
its execution history remains authoritative through recovery; once a funded
claim has earned payment, subsequent work cannot revoke its reserve.

By \emph{verifiable work}, we mean work with checkable authorization, an
attributable execution history and acceptance under agreed rules. Verification
establishes these facts under the selected authorities and acceptance procedure.
The quality of that procedure determines what acceptance says about the result.

The construction separates a task's identity from the version of the plan that
contains it. Checked extensions retain earlier allocations and execution rights.
Durable admission prevents a new graph signature or a restarted planner from
renewing a consumed right. Independently funded agreements preserve earned
claims through the failure of the larger program. We specify these mechanisms
and establish a conditional preservation result for their composition. The
implementation and evaluation follow delegated work through task discovery,
graph growth, process loss and settlement, and compare the financial rules with
transactional escrow and ERC-8183.
