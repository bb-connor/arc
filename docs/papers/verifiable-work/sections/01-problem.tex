\section{A kernel for the agentic web}
\label{sec:introduction}
An agentic program chooses its next steps while it runs. A result may reveal a
new task, a new collaborator or a need to delegate part of the work. Across
organizations, those choices touch resources with different owners: tools,
data, execution capacity and money. The program needs to compose them without
absorbing their owners into one administrative domain.

Consider an intermediary agent commissioned by a buyer to review an API.
It assigns an initial survey, then uses the returned result to hire a specialist.
The specialist works under
its own operator's policy and may finish before the intermediary does. If the
intermediary crashes, the specialist's completed work and earned payment must
have a meaning independent of the lost process. Meanwhile, the buyer needs to
know that adding the specialist neither exceeded its allocation nor
re-authorized an earlier invocation.

The difficulty is preserving commitments as the plan changes. An allocation
must not become two promises merely because two providers respond. A new graph
version must not turn a consumed continuation into another attempt. A signed
foreign request must still satisfy the receiving owner's policy. A refund must
not rewrite an uncertain tool effect or consume funds already owed to another
participant. These conditions span planning, authorization, execution and
settlement. They must agree on which work they describe.

Chio supplies that boundary. Its kernel mediates agent tool calls through
capabilities, guards, durable admission and signed receipts. This paper extends
that substrate into an execution contract for evolving work: delegate bounded
capacity, seal an exact invocation, admit it under the receiver's authority,
retain its execution identity, and discharge its financial obligation under
the agreed acceptance rule. The planner chooses the work; the resource owners
enforce the commitments.

The organizing principle is \emph{plans may evolve while issued commitments
retain their bounds and identity}. Before a selection is sealed, an agent can
choose another provider. After sealing, it must resolve the original work under
its original identity. A running swarm can add tasks within its fixed envelope,
while existing tasks keep their allocations and continuation resources.
An earned payment follows its own agreement even when its parent fails.

This is \emph{programmable sovereignty}: an owner expresses conditions for using
its resources, and a kernel applies those conditions before an effect crosses
the owner's boundary. Treaties make cooperation explicit without importing a
foreign participant's authority. Commitments give that cooperation a durable
meaning after the planner changes or disappears.

The contribution is the composition of these rules at the native invocation
boundary. We specify the authenticated bindings between delegated work, signed
swarm plans, receiver-owned admission and funded claims; establish their
preservation property under stated custody assumptions; and implement the
complete path in Rust. One executable trajectory runs initial work, discovers
and funds a collaborator, grows the graph, survives intermediary process loss,
and collects the collaborator's original earned claim.

The resulting system is peer-to-peer in its authority structure: participants
negotiate work and enforce their own admission conditions. Allocation and
settlement still have explicit authorities. Unlike Bitcoin's shared spend
history~\cite{bitcoin}, Chio requires no single global history for all programs.
Each scarce resource has an identified custodian, and every handoff binds the
same work to that custodian's decision.
