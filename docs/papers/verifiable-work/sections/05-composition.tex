\section{What composes}
The arguments concern transitions under the assumptions of Section 2.
They do not prove the complete Rust implementation or semantic correctness of
a task decomposition.

\subsection{Delegation invariants}
\begin{proposition}[Declared attenuation and bounded capacity]
In D1, every descendant's declared effects and readers are subsets of its
ancestors', its expiry is no later, and the sum of executable leaf ceilings is
at most the root ceiling.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Subset inclusion and the expiry order are transitive. Strictly decreasing
depth bounds the lineage. Let $U_v$ be the unallocated remainder of an internal
slot. Initially the root is one leaf of ceiling $B$. Subdivision moves $b$
units from a leaf or internal remainder into a child of ceiling $b$, atomically
and with a nonnegative remainder. Therefore
\[
 \sum_{\text{leaves }v} b_v+\sum_{\text{internal }v}U_v=B.
\]
Selection and sealing change no ceiling. Containers cannot execute, so only
leaf capacity can authorize invocations. Native enforcement of the bound
one-invocation capability in one protected receiver custody domain bounds
admitted charges by those ceilings. This last step assumes native enforcement;
the arithmetic alone does not prove it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Stable sealed identity]
After a D1 selection is sealed, an accepted permit cannot change its receiver,
capability, input, effect or request identity without violating an authenticated
binding or the trusted allocator's transition rules.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Selection revisions serialize replacements before sealing. The allocator
freezes the selection before exporting a permit and offers no later transition
that replaces it. Each receiver compares its invocation with that signed
selection and the complete slot digest. Substitution either fails a comparison
or requires a new trusted statement. Replaying identical evidence reaches
native custody under the original identity; it supplies no fresh capability.
\end{proof}

This result permits changing collaborators before commitment while preserving
uncertain obligations afterward. It trades reclaim and automatic failover for
a simple safety rule. A future reclaim protocol would need authoritative
closure or fencing of the original operation. Expiry or an agent's claim of
absence is insufficient. These D1 arguments are not mechanized; the retained
Lean results below concern the separate funded state machine.

\subsection{Commitments across graph versions}
Let $G_0,\ldots,G_n$ be the installed S1 lineage and $A_i$ its set of uniquely
identified allocations, with ceiling $b(a)$ for allocation $a$. Let $B$ be the
fixed declared pool. Every checked extension requires
\[
 A_i\subseteq A_{i+1},\qquad b_{i+1}(a)=b_i(a)\ (a\in A_i),
 \qquad\sum_{a\in A_{i+1}} b(a)\leq B.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Bounded commitments under additive growth]
Within one protected S1 lineage, the ceilings of all allocations ever installed
sum to at most $B$. Graph extension preserves an old task's authority artifacts
and cannot refresh its consumed native continuation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An atomic compare-and-swap admits at most one successor from each installed
head. Retention makes the allocation sets monotone, so
$\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}A_i=A_n$. The live verifier bounds the sum in $A_n$ by $B$;
checked integer arithmetic prevents overflow. The extension check retains
every old continuation ID and its allocation and forbids a second continuation
for that task or allocation. Native custody uses this retained ID across graph
versions. Re-signing its new graph digest cannot create a second native
resource. Exact historical resolution supplies the original artifacts to old
requests and physical-claim revalidation. Ordinary expiry, revocation and
local admission still govern whether unstarted work may execute.
\end{proof}

This argument covers interleaving extension with old and new dispatch because
growth neither removes an allocation nor resets its execution identity. It
requires a single protected head for the pool and a single native custody domain
for each task. A copied database or a second independently provisioned graph
does not inherit the exclusivity of the first. The result bounds declared
commitments; it supplies neither financial backing nor a proof of task meaning.
The native trajectory demonstrates conditional progress for a new worker and
an original unstarted worker, without asserting termination of an arbitrary
swarm. The S1 argument, like D1's, is not a mechanized Rust refinement.

\subsection{Funded obligations}

For each funding source $u$, let $D_u$ be its fixed deposited endowment,
$A_u$ its unallocated balance, $L_u$ its locked allocations, $P_u$ its paid
outflow and $R_u$ its refunded outflow. Returned cash remains recorded as an
outflow in this accounting interval; a later deposit opens a new interval.
The conservation invariant is
\begin{equation}
 D_u=A_u+L_u+P_u+R_u,\qquad A_u,L_u,P_u,R_u\geq0. \label{eq:conservation}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded funded exposure]
If reservation moves $q\leq A_u$ from available to locked funds, and payment
or refund moves an allocation's locked amount once to its corresponding
outflow, every reachable state satisfies (\ref{eq:conservation}). Total paid
and refunded outflow is at most the original deposit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The initial state is $(D_u,0,0,0)$. Reservation subtracts and adds the same
quantity within the sum. Payment and refund do the same across a different
pair of accounts. Their guards preserve nonnegativity. Other transitions do
not change money. Induction over the transition sequence proves the equality
and bound. This argument requires the implementation to identify allocations
uniquely and couple each state transition with its actual transfer.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Earned child independence]
Suppose each work edge has a distinct allocation with its own immutable terms,
an earned claim can only remain payable or become paid, and a transition on
one edge cannot modify another's allocation. Then any sequence of parent
failure, refund or completion transitions leaves an earned unpaid child
collectible under its original terms.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A transition concerning another edge leaves the child unchanged. A transition
on the child preserves the set $\{\textsf{Payable},\textsf{Paid}\}$. Induction
therefore excludes child refund after acceptance. Distinct funding prevents a
parent transfer from consuming the child's reserve. Eventual collection further
requires availability, an exercisable beneficiary key and transaction inclusion.
\end{proof}

A small Lean specification checks conservation over arbitrary finite account
traces, the paid-plus-refunded bound, persistence of the earned states, and
noninterference of distinct map entries. A finite Python explorer checks
concrete timing and role guards; selected traces are replayed against Solidity.
These are complementary checks. There is no machine-checked refinement from the
Rust kernel or Solidity contract to the Lean specification, and the arithmetic
proof does not authenticate a signer or prove that a token is honest.

The capital cost follows directly. If $E_{\rm open}$ contains the distinct
unsettled obligations, their reserve is $\sum_{e\in E_{\rm open}}q_e$. A chain of
promised future receipts cannot reduce this amount under F1. The example locks
160 units while the two obligations coexist, although the buyer's task costs
100. Short milestones may reduce capital duration; they do not eliminate
capital needs. Capital efficiency must be measured alongside success rate.

A payer-only signature cannot supply the missing exclusivity. Consider two
receivers that see individually valid promises against the same deposit and
have no common state or other non-equivocation mechanism. Each receiver's view
is indistinguishable from a run in which it is the only payee. If both accept
on that evidence alone, the two promises can exceed the deposit. The argument
identifies a missing shared fact; a conventional trusted ledger, a qualified
rollback-resistant authority or a settlement chain can supply it. It is not an
impossibility result about OAuth, A2A, capabilities or ordinary escrow.
