\section{Preservation of commitments}
\label{sec:preservation}
The construction must preserve two quantities and one identity: the capacity
allocated to work, the funds reserved for payment, and the invocation to which
each execution right belongs. We first state the accounting invariants, then
show why plan growth and recovery preserve them together.

\subsection{Delegated and planned capacity}
Let $B_D$ be a delegation root's ceiling, $b_v$ a leaf's ceiling and $U_v$ an
internal slot's unused remainder. The allocation tree satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\mathrm{leaves}\ v}b_v+\sum_{\mathrm{internal}\ v}U_v=B_D,
 \qquad b_v,U_v\geq0. \label{eq:delegation}
\end{equation}
Subdivision moves capacity from a parent into its children. Only leaves can
execute, so internal capacity cannot also be spent as a tool call. Selection
and sealing change no ceiling. Restrictions on operations and readers, expiry
and delegation depth hold transitively along the tree. The receiver's
one-invocation capability and charge checks bound each executed leaf.

For a graph $G_i$, let $\mathcal{A}_i$ be its set of uniquely identified
allocations and $b(a)$ the ceiling of allocation $a$. A checked extension
preserves
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{A}_i\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{i+1},\quad
 b_{i+1}(a)=b_i(a)\ (a\in\mathcal{A}_i),\quad
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{i+1}}b(a)\leq B_G, \label{eq:growth}
\end{equation}
where $B_G$ is the original declared pool. One protected sequence of graph
versions per pool, with serialized installation, gives this bound a single
accounting domain. Since allocations are retained,
$\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}\mathcal{A}_i=\mathcal{A}_n$. The latest bound therefore covers
every allocation installed, including work still running under an older graph.

\subsection{Deposited funds}
Consider one deposited amount $D_u$, accounted separately from later deposits.
Let $F_u$ be its available funds, $L_u$ its locked funds, and $P_u,R_u$ its paid
and refunded outflows. Then
\begin{equation}
 D_u=F_u+L_u+P_u+R_u,\qquad F_u,L_u,P_u,R_u\geq0.
 \label{eq:conservation}
\end{equation}
Reservation moves funds from $F_u$ to $L_u$. Payment or refund moves an
allocation's locked funds once to the corresponding outflow. Refunded cash
remains an outflow from this deposited amount even if deposited again. A new
deposit creates another accounting entry and leaves outstanding locks in the
earlier entry unchanged.

Acceptance restricts a claim to $\{\textsf{Payable},\textsf{Paid}\}$. A transition
on another allocation cannot change that claim or release its reserve.
This is the financial reason that a parent refund cannot revoke a child's
earned payment. Withdrawal also requires the beneficiary's key, available
evidence, transaction inclusion and a functioning asset.

\subsection{The combined guarantee}
\begin{proposition}[Preservation under plan growth and recovery]
Assume the protected allocation, graph, execution and settlement state of
Section~\ref{sec:sovereignty}, the authenticated references of
Section~\ref{sec:contract}, and the transitions of Section~\ref{sec:execution}.
Start with validly provisioned roots, verified initial graphs and funded
accounts. Every finite interleaving of valid subdivision, selection, sealing, graph
extension, execution, recovery and settlement has these properties:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Delegation and graph allocations remain within their original bounds;
       paid plus refunded funds do not exceed deposited backing.
 \item A sealed invocation retains its receiver, capability, input, tool operation
       and request. Plan growth and replay grant it no further execution right.
 \item An earned child claim retains its terms and remains payable or paid
       through parent failure, completion or refund.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Initially, a root has all its capacity in one leaf, a verified graph fits its
declared pool, and a fresh deposit has
$(F_u,L_u,P_u,R_u)=(D_u,0,0,0)$. No execution right has been consumed or claim
earned. The properties hold in this state. Induct over the permitted
transitions.

Subdivision preserves (\ref{eq:delegation}) by transferring capacity from the
parent remainder to a child. Selection does not alter capacity. Sealing freezes
the selected invocation before its permit can leave the allocator.

Graph extension retains all earlier allocations and checks (\ref{eq:growth})
before installing the successor. Existing continuation identifiers remain
unchanged. A receiver's record of their use is therefore unchanged as well.
The archive supplies the original graph and signature for an old request.
Concurrent installers serialize through the same protected head.

Admission checks that the permit, capability, task, route and treaty refer to
the presented invocation. The payment agreement commits to that complete
request. A substituted object fails a reference check or belongs to a different
authorized commitment. Before dispatch, the receiver durably assigns the
execution rights and budget hold to the original execution record. Replay
consults that assignment; it cannot acquire the same rights for another invocation.
Expiry, revocation and policy refusal may prevent a call but cannot create
a replacement right.

Recovery returns a stored permit or completed output when available. Otherwise,
an unknown execution outcome remains unknown and supplies no fresh authority
to call the tool. These steps create no allocation and release no consumed
execution right. Financial retries reuse the persisted transaction and its
allocation, subject to the same settlement checks as the original submission.

Reservation, payment and refund preserve (\ref{eq:conservation}) and affect only
their named allocations. Acceptance leaves the child in
$\{\textsf{Payable},\textsf{Paid}\}$ under every subsequent child transition.
A parent transition changes another allocation.

These cases remain valid when interleaved: each shared resource has a serializing
authority, and graph extension retains the resources named by earlier work.
The authenticated references require each authority's decision to concern the
same invocation, completing the induction.
\end{proof}

The argument depends on exclusive custody at each scarce resource. Two
receivers can each be shown a signed promise against one deposit while neither
sees the other promise. Each local view is consistent with an affordable
exchange. A protected allocator or ledger must establish that both promises
have not spent the same capacity; signatures alone cannot do so.

The guarantee is safety under the specified transitions. A missing later record
may prevent progress, but it cannot undo an earlier commitment. This is why
separate local transactions suffice even when a handoff fails partway through.

The accompanying Lean development checks abstract financial conservation and
earned-state lemmas. Tests exercise the concrete admission and recovery rules.
The full argument above is conditional on those rules; the artifact does not
contain a machine-checked refinement from the argument to the Rust and Solidity
implementations.
