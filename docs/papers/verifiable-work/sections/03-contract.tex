\section{The work contract}
Write a D1 slot as
\[
 S=(i,h,E,A,b,c,t,d,Q),\qquad s=H(\operatorname{canon}(S)).
\]
$i$ identifies the slot, $h$ its holder, $E$ its exact server/tool pairs, $A$
its permitted reader keys, $b$ its ceiling in currency $c$, $t$ its expiry,
$d$ its remaining depth and $Q$ its acceptance predicate. The owner creates a
root through a trusted configuration API. Each allocator has a persistent
namespace $N$. The allocation digest $a=H(\operatorname{canon}(N,S_{\rm root},S))$
binds both the full root and the target slot within that namespace, under a
versioned domain. A holder signs a subdivision naming the parent, its allocation
digest and the complete child. The allocator admits it only when that digest
matches its protected state and
\begin{align*}
 E_{\rm child}&\subseteq E_{\rm parent}, &
 A_{\rm child}&\subseteq A_{\rm parent},\\
 t_{\rm child}&\leq t_{\rm parent}, &
 d_{\rm child}&<d_{\rm parent},\\
 c_{\rm child}&=c_{\rm parent}, &
 \sum_{j\in\operatorname{children}(S)} b_j&\leq b_S.
\end{align*}
The child must still be live. Its holder and selected receiver must be in its
reader set. Immediate SQLite transactions couple insertion with the parent's
allocation counter. A slot with children cannot execute; a selected slot
cannot acquire children. Unused internal capacity remains available for
additional children. There is no reclaim operation in D1.

Children perform different subtasks and may have different predicates. A
successful child therefore does not establish that its parent task succeeded.
A separate leaf may assemble results and check the final predicate within its
own allocation. D1 supplies capacity-preserving subdivision, not automatic
semantic decomposition of an owner's goal.

\subsection{Offer, selection and commitment}
A receiver signs an offer binding the allocation digest $a$, complete slot digest
$s$, its own key,
the exact effect and input digest, a price ceiling and an expiry. Binding the
complete slot is essential: signing only its name would allow the offer to be
transplanted into different acceptance terms. Binding $a$ additionally prevents
replay between honest owners using equal local slot names and terms. The holder
signs a selection
containing that offer, the original native request ID, the receiver-issued
capability digest and the expected selection revision. Compare-and-swap
revisions permit replacement and reject stale selections.

Sealing freezes the selected binding before the allocator exports a signed
\emph{dispatch permit}. The permit contains the root ID, allocation digest, immutable slot,
signed selection and issuance time. An accepted allocator signature attests
that the slot was allocated and sealed; the receiver does not independently
reconstruct the owner's entire allocation tree. It checks this evidence
against its configured allocator keys, while its native capability supplies
the actual authority to execute.

Every signed body has its own versioned domain and uses RFC 8785 canonical
JSON~\cite{jcs}. The input digest covers the slot ID and exact payload. It
excludes the enclosing permit to avoid circular hashing; the permit has its
own signature. Unknown wrapper fields reject. Wire numbers are limited to
$2^{53}-1$, depth to 32, children per slot to 64 and slots per root to 4,096.
The acceptance language is a conjunction of 1--16 exact JSON-pointer
equalities or inclusive integer ranges. These limits define the implemented
profile, rather than a general language for proving useful computation.

\subsection{An allocation ceiling and a funded obligation}
F1 binds an agreement digest, payer, beneficiary, verifier, asset, amount and
deadlines to an actual reserved allocation. The allocation identifier includes
the chain and contract address. A new payer agreement cannot be funded twice
under the same payer and digest. The receiver relies on a qualified observation
of that reserve, not a signed balance supplied by the payer.

Acceptance in F1 is a separate EIP-712 decision~\cite{eip712} binding the
allocation, agreement, submission, decision digest, acceptance bit, beneficiary,
asset and amount. The escrow authenticates the pinned verifier; it does not
execute the application checker. A missing output or custody record yields no
decision. An available result that fails the predicate yields rejection.

A child agreement is separately funded. In the 100/60 example, the intermediary
must already control the child's 60; the buyer's reserved 100 cannot be pledged
again. D1's 100-unit allocation tree, by comparison, divides one owner's
authority ceiling. It does not promise both a 100-unit parent refund and
additional child payments from the same 100. Credit, conditional netting and
cross-profile deployment require their own backing and integration rules.
