\section{From authority to a work commitment}
\label{sec:contract}
The work commitment is the unit of composition. It develops through an ordered
set of decisions: allocate capacity, choose an offer, seal the invocation, back
its payment, and admit it at the receiver. The implementation uses existing
signed records for these decisions. Their authenticated bindings make them
refer to one piece of work.

\subsection{Delegate bounded capacity}
A work slot contains
\[
 S=(i,h,E,A,b,c,t,d,Q),
\]
where $i$ is its identity, $h$ its holder, $E$ its permitted server/tool pairs,
$A$ its permitted readers, $b$ its ceiling in currency $c$, $t$ its expiry,
$d$ its remaining delegation depth, and $Q$ its acceptance predicate.
The owner creates a root through a trusted configuration API. A holder signs
a subdivision naming its parent allocation and the complete child slot.

The allocator admits a child only when its effects and readers are subsets of
the parent's, its expiry is no later, its depth is smaller, and its currency is
unchanged. All child ceilings together must fit within the parent's ceiling:
\[
 \sum_{j\in\operatorname{children}(S)} b_j\leq b_S.
\]
An atomic transaction couples the new child with the parent's allocation
counter. A slot that has children cannot execute; a selected slot cannot acquire
children. Unused internal capacity remains available for further subdivision.
This prevents the same capacity from authorizing both a container and its leaves.

An allocation digest binds the allocator's persistent namespace, the complete
root and the complete slot. Equal local names at different honest allocators
therefore identify different allocations. A child can specify a different
predicate because it performs a different task. The parent's result still
requires its own check.

\subsection{Select, then seal}
A provider signs an offer over the allocation and slot digests, its receiving
key, the exact effect and input, a price ceiling and an expiry. The holder
selects that offer together with the original request ID and the digest of the
receiver-issued capability. Revision checks serialize replacements while the
selection remains open.

Sealing freezes the selection. The allocator persists a signed dispatch permit
before returning it. That permit carries the immutable slot and signed
selection, allowing the receiver to authenticate the commitment without a live
connection to the allocator. It relies on the allocator's protected state for
exclusive allocation and on the receiver's own custody for execution.

The receiver compares the permit with the actual subject, receiver, capability,
tool, input and request. Its native grant authorizes one exact invocation with
monetary ceilings no greater than the offer. The receiver also checks validity
and current local authorization. A caller cannot substitute a different
provider or input while retaining the original consent.

\subsection{Back the obligation}
Funded work adds an agreement that binds the complete final request, payer,
beneficiary, verifier, asset, amount, settlement domain and deadlines. A
qualified observation must establish that the agreement's allocation holds the
required funds. The escrow identifier includes its chain and contract domain;
a new agreement cannot reuse another allocation's reserve.

Each subcontract has its own backing. In the running example, the
intermediary pays for the specialist from funds it already controls. Its
expected payment from the buyer remains locked under a different agreement.
This choice assigns the cost of a failed parent to the participant that hired
the child, while preserving the child's separately earned claim.

\subsection{Bind the handoffs}
The composed receiver checks that the delegated slot, native request and swarm
task have the same identity. The swarm allocation names the delegated
allocation digest and agrees on amount and currency. The sealed permit and
swarm witness bind the same native capability, and the signed route targets
this receiver.

The funded agreement then authenticates the complete request, including its
delegation, graph and treaty references. Its financial allocation is a distinct
identifier linked through that agreement. The hash chain has no cycle: the
request contains the admission references; the agreement commits to the
request; the escrow commits to the agreement.

Native signed bodies use canonical JSON and versioned domains~\cite{jcs}.
Settlement decisions use the domain-bound EIP-712 encoding described in
Section~\ref{sec:execution}.
Admission checks these relationships as well as each individual signature.
Individually valid objects that refer to different work do not compose.
The protected receiver profile installs the same checks before fresh work and
recovery, so stripping request context or local configuration cannot select
a weaker admission path.
