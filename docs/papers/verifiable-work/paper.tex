\begin{abstract}
Autonomous agents must coordinate work across systems whose owners retain
control of their tools, data and funds. We present Chio, a kernel architecture
for peer-to-peer verifiable work. Chio separates agent planning from the
authority and durable execution state governing its effects. Each resource
owner admits work under locally controlled capabilities, policies and treaties.
Linked commitments bind a selected invocation to its delegated resources,
receiving authority and conditions of acceptance. A program grows by adding
tasks while preserving the commitments of previously authorized work. Recovery
retains the original execution history, and separately funded agreements
preserve payments earned by accepted work. Signed receipts carry evidence of
these decisions between participating domains. Under explicit trust assumptions,
we establish preservation of resource bounds, invocation identity and earned
claims through plan growth and recovery. Chio's kernel supplies the authority
and recovery mechanisms; a fixed-profile adapter composes their work-admission
bindings. Evaluation under one administrator exercises plan growth, process
loss and settlement. The architecture provides an execution foundation for the
agentic web: agents can select collaborators as their plans
develop, while each owner enforces the conditions governing its resources and
prior commitments.
\end{abstract}
