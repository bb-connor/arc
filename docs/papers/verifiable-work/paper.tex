\begin{abstract}
An agentic web requires programs that can recruit collaborators, delegate work
and spend resources across organizational boundaries. Those programs must be
free to change their plans while remaining bound by the commitments they
issue. We present Chio, a kernel for composing agentic work under the authority
of its resource owners. Chio binds delegated capacity, a selected invocation,
receiver-local admission, execution evidence and funded payment into one
verifiable chain of commitments. Each receiver enforces its own capabilities
and treaty conditions. A running program can add tasks within its original
allocation; earlier work retains its terms, execution identity and continuation
custody. Recorded earned payments remain collectible under their agreed terms
after the parent fails. We give the protocol, a conditional preservation
argument and a Rust implementation. In a composed local execution, a first
result triggers a newly funded collaborator, the intermediary is killed after
that collaborator earns payment, and the original claim is collected while
earlier execution evidence remains unchanged. Chio makes programmable sovereignty
an execution rule: owners control admission, programs compose through explicit
commitments, and changes to the plan preserve obligations already incurred.
\end{abstract}
