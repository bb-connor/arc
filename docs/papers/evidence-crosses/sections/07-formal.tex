\section{What is mechanized}
\label{sec:formal}

The decision core is a pure function: verify a capability, resolve grants,
evaluate, sign. It is modeled in Lean 4 \cite{lean4}, whose published theorem
inventory carries \PSTheoremCount{} declarations and one explicit axiom, a
collision-resistance idealization registered against ASSUME-SHA256. No serializer, protocol step, or kernel behavior is axiomatized;
an unproved property is an assumption-registry entry.

Two results bear on this paper. Occurrence theorems make the predicate language
fail closed: an atom unsupported, or undefined for the admission view, anywhere
in a predicate forces denial on every view, by induction. The second is the
comparison set of Section~\ref{sec:security}: a classification of every leaf of the co-signed
predicate is total by exhaustiveness over the leaf type, and modelled acceptance
is a statement gate conjoined with the named comparisons, so acceptance pins
every equality-bound leaf, puts the two set-bound leaves in their receiver-held
sets, and survives replacing an uncompared leaf with a value of its declared
shape.

Completeness is cross-checked, not transcribed: the executed leaf corpus derives
its enumeration from the wire type, reads the Lean module, and fails if the leaf
paths or the classifications disagree.

\dfn{Lean does not verify the running Rust.} These theorems are about the
modelled predicate and the classification; the corpus is what links them to the
shipped hook, on one fixture. There are four kinds of tripwire, each failing
loudly when the two drift apart: named Rust functions mirrored to Lean
definitions by content hash and checked on every change; \PSAeneasFunctionCount{} production functions
extracted through a Rust-to-Lean pipeline with equivalence theorems that must
rebuild byte-identically; \PSKaniHarnessCount{} bounded model-checking harnesses
over public entry points, one rejecting an untrusted issuer before dispatch; and
the cross-language encoder corpus of Section~\ref{sec:eval}.

We report survivors, not percentages. Of \PSLeanMutants{} mutants in the Lean
development \PSLeanMutantsKilled{} were killed and \PSLeanMutantsSurvived{}
survived, each with an open issue, and of \PSProofMutants{} in the Rust
proof-facing sources \PSProofMutantsKilled{} were killed,
\PSProofMutantsUnviable{} were unviable, and \PSProofMutantsSurvived{} survived.
A survivor is a place where a theorem is weaker than it looks.
