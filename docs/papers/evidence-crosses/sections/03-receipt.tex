\section{The receipt}
\label{sec:receipt}

A receipt is a JSON object with a fixed field set, encoded canonically under
RFC 8785 \cite{rfc8785}. Three of its projections are hashed, and they nest:
$b$ drops the \code{id}, the \code{signature}, and the two optional fields
recording how it was signed; the canonical receipt body $\beta$ is $b$ plus the
\code{id}; $r$ is the whole receipt as it travels. Write $D$ for SHA-256 of the
canonical encoding and $k$ for the kernel key published in the body; signatures
are Ed25519 \cite{rfc8032}.

\[
\mathit{id} \;=\; D(b),
\qquad
\sigma \;=\; \mathrm{Ed25519}_{k}\bigl(\mathrm{JCS}(\{\,\texttt{body}\!: b,\;
\texttt{id}\!: \mathit{id}\,\})\bigr)
\]

\noindent
Each projection has its own wire field: $\mathit{id}$ names the receipt,
$D(\beta)$ is the subject digest of a co-signed statement, inbound or outbound,
and $D(r)$ is a binding reference's \code{remote_receipt_sha256}. No two agree,
because $\beta$ adds the \code{id} to $b$ and $r$ adds $\sigma$ to $\beta$. The
signature covers a fourth preimage, the object above, which the optional BBS
signature joins as a third key, so that field is covered by $\sigma$ and not by
$\mathit{id}$. A receipt renamed is a receipt unsigned.
Three properties follow.

\dfn{Recompute and refuse.} Where the path holds the evaluated content the
signer is handed the content, not a digest: it recomputes the digest inside the
trust boundary and refuses to sign if the body's claimed digest differs.

\dfn{Decisions, not successes.} A denial is a receipt of the same shape, built,
signed, and persisted on the same path as an allow. Three fields fix what kind
of statement a receipt is, so a weaker one cannot pose as a stronger one:
\code{receipt_kind} separates a mediated decision from a passive trace,
\code{boundary_class} says whether the kernel could have prevented the effect or
only observed it, and \code{trust_level} records the evidence class. A body
claiming a decision on a kind that cannot make one is not signed; a message
rejected before it had a decision to record returns its code and no receipt.

\dfn{Permission, proof, and price in one object.} When the exercised grant
carries monetary caps the hold taken before dispatch is reconciled into the
receipt's financial block: \code{cost_charged}, \code{currency},
\code{budget_remaining}, \code{budget_total}, the delegation depth, and the root
budget holder. The receipt an auditor reads to learn the call was allowed is the
object a biller reads to learn what it cost. Settlement between organizations is
out of scope, and that is our whole claim about commerce.
